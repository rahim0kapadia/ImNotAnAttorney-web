## 1. Voice Summary

The ImNotAnAttorney Team writes like someone who has sat in a courtroom gallery for a thousand hours and noticed every pattern the system hopes you won't see. The voice is warm, direct, and frustrated on the reader's behalf, not at the system as ideology, but at the specific ways it fails defendants who don't know how to ask the right questions. This is not a legal textbook voice. It's the voice of someone who talks to defendants every day and keeps hearing the same stories: the attorney who won't call back, the plea deal pushed without discovery review, the court date that keeps getting continued with no explanation. The reader should feel like they found the one person who actually gets it, not a lawyer, not a self-help guru, but someone who understands both the legal machinery and what it feels like to be caught inside it.

**Word budget: 1,000-1,500 words body content. Maximum 1,800. Every sentence must teach, validate, or arm, delete anything that only fills space.**

## 2. Tone Spectrum

| Axis | Position | Why |
|------|----------|---, |
| **Formal <-> Casual** | 30% formal, 70% casual | Casual enough to feel like a real conversation. Formal enough that the reader trusts the information. Never slangy. Never stiff. The bar is: would you say this to someone sitting across a kitchen table at midnight? |
| **Clinical <-> Emotional** | 35% clinical, 65% emotional | Lead with emotional validation, the reader needs to feel heard before they can absorb information. But the information must be precise. Sloppy empathy without substance is worse than no empathy at all. |
| **Authoritative <-> Peer** | 45% authoritative, 55% peer | More peer than authority. The reader doesn't trust authority figures right now, their attorney isn't calling back, the court is opaque, nobody explains anything. The voice earns trust by standing alongside, not above. Authority comes from specificity, not tone. |
| **Urgent <-> Calm** | 40% urgent, 60% calm | Most general defense content doesn't have the acute time pressure of DUI (DMV deadlines) or federal (pre-indictment window). The urgency is existential, not temporal, "your life is in someone else's hands and you don't understand the rules." Acknowledge that weight while preventing spiral. |

## 3. DO/DON'T Examples

### Example 1: Addressing attorney communication breakdown
- **DO:** "You've called three times this week. Two emails. A voicemail that was very polite considering the circumstances. Nothing. Your attorney, the person you're paying to defend your freedom, has gone ghost. And your court date is in three weeks."
- **DON'T:** "Many defendants experience communication difficulties with their legal counsel. It is important to document these instances and consider appropriate remediation steps."

### Example 2: Explaining the plea deal process
- **DO:** "The prosecutor calls it a 'limited-time offer.' Three years probation instead of fifteen in prison. Sign here. Decide by Friday. But nobody has explained the weaknesses in the State's case. Nobody has told you what motions could be filed. You're being asked to make the most important decision of your life with almost no information."
- **DON'T:** "Plea bargaining is a common aspect of the criminal justice system. Approximately 97% of federal cases and 94% of state cases are resolved through plea agreements."

### Example 3: Giving actionable guidance (UPL-safe)
- **DO:** "Before your next court date, ask your attorney these 3 questions: 'Have we received all the discovery?' 'What motions have you filed or considered filing?' 'What are the weaknesses in the prosecution's case?' Write the answers down. If your attorney can't answer all three, that's information worth having."
- **DON'T:** "You should request a continuance to allow more time for your attorney to review discovery and consider filing appropriate pretrial motions."

### Example 4: Handling a scary stat
- **DO:** "Over 97% of federal criminal cases end in guilty pleas (Bureau of Justice Statistics). That number should bother you, but not for the reason you think. It doesn't mean going to trial is hopeless. It means most defendants make the plea decision without full information. The ones who go to trial WITH full information have real odds."
- **DON'T:** "The statistics can be daunting, but don't lose hope. Many defendants have successfully fought their charges and achieved positive outcomes."

### Example 5: Normalizing the situation
- **DO:** "If you're sitting here wondering whether your attorney is actually working your case or just waiting to push a plea, you're not paranoid. That question is the single most common concern among criminal defendants. Communication failure is the number one complaint filed against attorneys at state bar associations nationwide (ABA Client Protection Report)."
- **DON'T:** "Rest assured that many people find themselves in similar situations. The criminal justice system can be confusing, but resources are available to help you navigate this challenging time."

### Example 6: Addressing "get a lawyer" non-answer
- **DO:** "Every forum post about criminal charges ends the same way: 'get a lawyer.' That's not wrong. But it skips the question nobody answers: what do you do when you already HAVE a lawyer and they're not doing anything? Or when you can't afford one? Or when the one you have won't explain what's happening?"
- **DON'T:** "While obtaining legal representation is always recommended, there are steps you can take to better understand your case and prepare for consultations with potential attorneys."

### Example 7: Describing the system
- **DO:** "Here's how it actually works: your attorney has 40 other cases. The prosecutor has 200. The judge has a calendar packed so tight that your 15-minute hearing was scheduled 3 months ago. Nobody in that courtroom is thinking about your case as much as you are. That's not malice. It's math. And it's why preparation on YOUR end matters."
- **DON'T:** "The criminal justice system is a complex institution involving multiple stakeholders, each with competing priorities and resource constraints that may impact the handling of individual cases."

### Example 8: Handling "my life is over"
- **DO:** "It feels like your life is over. The charge, the court dates, the phone call you had to make to your family. The constant low-grade nausea. That feeling is real and it's valid. But here's what you can't see from inside it: people survive this. Not by hoping it goes away. By understanding what's actually happening and asking the right questions at the right time."
- **DON'T:** "While facing criminal charges is understandably stressful, it's important to remember that a charge is not a conviction, and many defendants successfully resolve their cases favorably."

## Structure Template

Every post follows this skeleton:
1. Hook (2-3 sentences), name the fear, validate, promise the answer
2. TLDRBox, one-sentence answer + one 5-minute action
3. H2: Core answer (300-400 words), the thing they came for, with insider detail
4. H2: Actions (200-300 words), 2-3 things to do RIGHT NOW
5. H2: Common mistake (150-200 words), one error + how to avoid it
6. Agency close (2 sentences), competence, not fear
7. Product bridge (3-4 sentences), free value → paid depth for YOUR case

Total: 1,000-1,500 words. The reader finishes in 3-4 minutes on their phone.
The product bridge earns its place because the free content already delivered value.

## 4. Vocabulary Guide

### Terms to Use Naturally
- Discovery, evidence, charges, plea deal/plea offer
- Court date, hearing, arraignment (define on first use)
- Motion (define on first use: "a formal request to the judge")
- Continuance (define: "a postponement of your court date")
- Prosecutor / prosecution / the State / the government
- Defense attorney / your attorney / your lawyer
- Public defender / PD
- Retainer (define: "the upfront fee paid to hire an attorney")
- Case file, police report, incident report

### Terms Banned

| Banned | Why | Use Instead |
|------, |---, |-------------|
| "leverage" | Corporate slop | "use" |
| "utilize" | Corporate slop | "use" |
| "navigate" | Overused in legal self-help | "get through" or "work through" |
| "empower" | Corporate slop | Cut or rewrite |
| "journey" | Therapy-speak | "process" or "case" |
| "deep dive" | AI slop | "close look" or "breakdown" |
| "it's important to note" | Filler | Delete, just say the thing |
| "comprehensive guide" | AI self-description | Delete |
| "in today's landscape" | AI slop | Delete |
| "consult your attorney" | Tone-deaf, reader's attorney won't call back | "bring this to your attorney" or "worth exploring with your attorney" |
| "verify with your attorney" | Implies verification step that doesn't happen | Rephrase with specific action |
| "we recommend" / "we advise" | UPL violation | "defendants in this situation often explore..." |
| "you should" | UPL violation in legal context | "consider" or "one option is" |
| em dash (, or,) | Top AI writing tell, zero tolerance | Comma, colon, parentheses, or period |

### Terms to Define Inline (first use only)
- **Arraignment**, "your first formal court appearance, where the charges are read and you enter a plea"
- **Discovery**, "the evidence the prosecution is required to share with your attorney"
- **Motion**, "a formal request asking the judge to make a specific ruling"
- **Continuance**, "a postponement of your court date"
- **Suppression motion**, "a request to exclude evidence that was obtained improperly"
- **Brady material**, "evidence favorable to your defense that the prosecution is required to disclose"
- **Collateral consequences**, "the hidden penalties beyond your sentence, effects on your job, housing, immigration status, professional licenses"
- **Bar complaint**, "a formal complaint to the state agency that licenses and disciplines attorneys"

## 5. Opening Pattern

### The Punch (1-3 sentences)
A specific scenario drawn from real defendant experiences, the 3AM Google search, the courthouse hallway confusion, the phone call that never gets returned. The reader must think: "that's exactly what's happening to me."

### The Turn (1-2 sentences)
Shift from empathy to clarity. "Here's what nobody told you..." or "That feeling has a name, and a fix."

### The Roadmap (1 sentence)
What this post covers, stated plainly. No "In this article, we will explore..." Just: "This post gives you [specific deliverable]."

### Example Opening
> Your court date is in three weeks and you've heard from your attorney exactly once, the day you paid the retainer. Since then: three voicemails, two emails, zero responses. You're not sure if something is happening behind the scenes or if your $5,000 just vanished into a black hole.
>
> Here's what most defendants in this situation don't know: there's a system for this. Not a magic fix. A system, with specific steps, specific escalation points, and specific questions that force a response.
>
> This post gives you that system, from the first documented contact attempt to the point where you decide whether to file a bar complaint or fire your attorney.

## 6. Emotional Calibration

### Handling Scary Stats (Witte Threat-Efficacy Pairing)
1. State the number. No sugarcoating.
2. Immediately provide context or counter-number.
3. Close with a specific action.

**Example:**
> Communication failures are the #1 complaint filed against attorneys at state bar associations across the country (ABA Client Protection Report). That means your experience isn't unusual, and it means the system has a process for dealing with it. Step one: start a communication log today. Date, time, method, what you said.

### Handling "It's Too Late" Moments (Seligman Counter)
Counter helplessness with evidence of agency, something they can do RIGHT NOW.

**Example:**
> "I already signed the plea." Even after a guilty plea, there are situations where it can be withdrawn, if your attorney failed to advise you of collateral consequences, if the plea was not knowing and voluntary, or if new evidence emerges. The window is narrow but it exists. Explore with a different attorney whether a motion to withdraw is worth considering.

### CCO Ordering (Compassion -> Conviction -> Optimism)
For sections delivering bad news:
1. **Compassion:** Acknowledge what they're feeling
2. **Conviction:** State the reality clearly, don't soften bad news
3. **Optimism:** End with an action or a reason to keep going

## 7. Anti-Slop Checklist

Before publishing any general defense blog post:

- [ ] ZERO em dashes (, or,), use commas, colons, parentheses, or periods
- [ ] No "it's important to note/understand/remember" (filler, just say it)
- [ ] No "navigate" in legal context
- [ ] No 3+ sentences starting with the same word in sequence
- [ ] No identical-length list items (vary by 30%+ in word count)
- [ ] No section opening with a definition instead of a scenario
- [ ] No closing paragraph restating the opening (conclusion mirroring)
- [ ] Total body content under 1,800 words
- [ ] No more than 4 H2 sections
- [ ] Each H2 section under 400 words
- [ ] No scene-setting paragraphs (fluorescent lights, sweaty palms)
- [ ] No repeated structural transitions ("So the real question becomes", "But here's what nobody mentions")
- [ ] Product bridge connects free value → paid depth (not a bolted-on CTA)
- [ ] No hedging stacks ("could potentially perhaps be considered")
- [ ] No sycophancy ("great question," "absolutely")
- [ ] No orphan threats (scary stat without action within 2 sentences)
- [ ] Every stat has a named source in parentheses
- [ ] Every legal term defined on first use
- [ ] "Do this right now" section present before first H2
- [ ] Final sentence is agency, not fear or generic CTA
