### Section 4: The Incentive Problem on Both Sides (5:30-7:00)

**[5:30-6:15] THE PD INCENTIVE**
[Text on screen: "THE INCENTIVE PROBLEM"]
"Both sides have incentive problems. Just different ones."

"The public defender's incentive problem is time. They don't have enough of it. The pressure isn't toward bad outcomes -- it's toward quick outcomes. Continuances, quick pleas, minimal investigation -- not because they want to, but because they physically don't have time for more."

**[6:15-7:00] THE PRIVATE INCENTIVE**
[PATTERN INTERRUPT: visual shift, different background/framing]
[Text on screen: "The incentive flip"]
"The private attorney's incentive problem is economic. Once you pay the flat retainer, every additional hour of work costs the attorney without generating additional revenue. The economic incentive flips toward minimum necessary effort."

"A quick plea is profitable. It closes the file, frees the calendar, opens space for the next retainer. A three-month suppression fight followed by trial? Expensive. Risky. And may not generate one more dollar."

"Neither incentive is evil. Neither is inevitable. But both exist, and understanding them is how you protect yourself regardless of who represents you."

### Section 6: The 5 Questions That Matter (8:00-9:30)

**[8:00-8:15] INTRO**
[Text on screen: "5 QUESTIONS THAT MATTER MORE THAN THE PRICE TAG"]
[Direct to camera]
"Here are five questions to ask any attorney -- public defender or private -- that tell you more about the quality of your representation than the price tag ever will."

**[8:15-9:30] THE QUESTIONS**
[Each question appears on screen as spoken]
"One: how many active cases are you currently handling? This tells you whether they have the bandwidth for yours. A public defender with 400 cases will answer honestly. A private attorney with 80 might not."

"Two: who will actually be working my case day to day? Some private firms assign cases to junior associates or paralegals. You need to know who's doing the actual work."

"Three: what is your trial experience with cases like mine? Trial preparation creates better plea deals. An attorney who never goes to trial gets worse offers."

"Four: what does the fee cover -- and what costs extra? Some retainers cover everything. Some don't cover investigators, experts, or trial. Know what you're paying for."

"Five: if my case has a viable suppression issue, will you file the motion even if it means more work? This is the question that separates attorneys who fight from attorneys who process."

[Direct to camera, pause]
"Watch how they respond to that last one. The answer tells you everything."
