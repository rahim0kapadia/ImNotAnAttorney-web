### Section 2: The Critical Rule (3:00-3:30)

**[3:00-3:30] DO NOT TALK**
[Text on screen in red: "DO NOT TALK TO FEDERAL AGENTS WITHOUT AN ATTORNEY."]
[Direct to camera, deliberate]

"Under 18 U.S.C. Section 1001, making a false statement to a federal agent is itself a federal crime -- even if the underlying investigation leads nowhere. 'I don't recall' said wrong becomes a false statement charge. 'I think it was...' becomes an admission."

"Silence is your right. The only response: 'I'd like to speak with my attorney before answering any questions.' Then stop talking."

### Section 4: Wire Fraud -- What the Government Must Prove (5:00-6:30)

**[5:00-5:30] THE ELEMENTS**
[Text on screen: "WIRE FRAUD: 18 U.S.C. 1343"]
"Wire fraud is the most commonly charged federal white collar crime. Twenty years per count. Thirty years per count if a financial institution is involved."

"But the government doesn't just have to prove that money moved. They have to prove four things."

[On-screen list appearing]
"One: a scheme to defraud. Two: specific intent to defraud. Three: use of interstate wire communications in furtherance of the scheme. Four: that money or property was obtained or attempted to be obtained."

**[5:30-6:00] INTENT IS EVERYTHING**
[PATTERN INTERRUPT: emphasis shift, closer framing]
[Text on screen: "INTENT is where cases are won."]
"Intent is the element that most wire fraud cases live and die on. Business deals go sideways all the time. Projections don't pan out. Promises get broken. None of that is automatically fraud."

"The government has to prove you KNEW what you were saying was false AT THE TIME YOU SAID IT -- and that you said it specifically to cheat someone. That's a high bar. But it's a bar your attorney has to know how to exploit."

**[6:00-6:30] GOOD FAITH DEFENSE**
[Text on screen: "Good faith belief = no intent = no fraud"]
"The good faith defense. If you genuinely believed your representations were true -- even if they turned out to be wrong -- that negates the intent element. Period."

"This isn't about whether your beliefs were reasonable. It's about whether they were sincerely held. Your attorney should be gathering every piece of evidence that shows what you actually believed at the time: internal communications, business plans, actions inconsistent with someone running a scam."

### Section 5: Federal Sentencing Guidelines (6:30-8:00)

**[6:30-7:15] HOW THE MATH WORKS**
[Text on screen: "FEDERAL SENTENCING GUIDELINES"]
[B-roll: sentencing chart, calculator imagery]
"Federal sentencing is not like state sentencing. It's built on a mathematical formula. Understanding that formula is critical to understanding your exposure."

"It starts with a base offense level. Then enhancements are added or subtracted."

[On-screen breakdown]
"Loss amount -- this has the biggest impact. Losses over $550,000 can add 14 or more levels to your guideline range."
"Number of victims -- more than 10 adds levels. More than 50 adds more."
"Role in the offense -- were you the leader, organizer, or a minimal participant?"
"Sophisticated means -- did the scheme use complex methods designed to avoid detection?"
"Obstruction -- did you destroy evidence, lie to agents, or interfere with the investigation?"

**[7:15-8:00] THE GUIDELINES TABLE**
[PATTERN INTERRUPT: sentencing table graphic]
[Text on screen: "Same charge. Wildly different sentences."]
"The sentencing guidelines table cross-references your total offense level with your criminal history category to produce a sentencing range. The difference between offense level 15 and offense level 25 can be the difference between probation and five years in federal prison."

"This is why loss calculations matter so much. Your attorney should be challenging the government's loss calculation aggressively. How are they calculating loss? Are they using intended loss or actual loss? Are they including losses that weren't caused by the alleged fraud? Each point of disagreement changes the math."

"Ask your attorney: what do you project my guidelines range to be? What are the specific enhancements the government will argue? How are we challenging the loss calculation?"

### Section 6: The Cooperation Trap (8:00-10:00)

**[8:00-8:30] THE PITCH**
[Text on screen: "THE COOPERATION TRAP"]
[Direct to camera, serious]
"The prosecutor leans back and says: 'If you cooperate, we can help you at sentencing.' Sounds great, right? You talk, they reduce your sentence, everybody wins."

"Except cooperation in a federal case is nothing like what most defendants imagine."

**[8:30-9:15] PROFFER SESSIONS**
[PATTERN INTERRUPT: dramatic lighting shift]
[Text on screen: "The Proffer Session ('Queen for a Day')"]
"A proffer session is the government's audition for your cooperation. You sit down with prosecutors and agents. You sign a proffer agreement. Then you talk."

"The proffer agreement says your WORDS can't be used directly against you at trial. That sounds protective. It's not."

"Derivative use is fair game. You mention a bank account in the Cayman Islands -- they can't play back your tape. But they can go find the account and use those records against you. You just gave them a roadmap to evidence they didn't have."

"Impeachment is fair game. If you testify at trial and say anything that contradicts what you said in the proffer -- they use your proffer statements to destroy your credibility."

"Lies void the agreement entirely. If the government determines you lied about anything -- even something minor -- everything you said becomes admissible. All of it."

**[9:15-10:00] THE SAFETY VALVE**
[Text on screen: "Safety Valve: 18 U.S.C. 3553(f)"]
"There's also the safety valve provision -- which allows judges to sentence below mandatory minimums for certain offenses if you meet specific criteria. Minimal criminal history. No violence or weapons. Not a leader or organizer. And truthful disclosure to the government."

"The safety valve is different from cooperation. You don't have to testify against anyone. You don't have to wear a wire. You just have to tell the truth about your own conduct."

"But the truthfulness requirement is absolute. If the government believes you withheld information or weren't fully candid, the safety valve is gone."

[Direct to camera]
"Before you agree to cooperate or proffer, your attorney must explain exactly what is protected, what isn't, and what the risks are. If they can't walk you through the mechanics -- they haven't done this enough times."
