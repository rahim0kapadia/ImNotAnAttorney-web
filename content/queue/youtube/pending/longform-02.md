**[0:30-1:00] MAKE SURE YOU HAVE IT**
[Text on screen: "Step 1: Do you actually have your discovery?"]

"Ask your attorney three things. Have you received discovery from the prosecution? Can I have a copy of everything? Is there anything outstanding that hasn't been turned over yet?"

### Section 2: The Police Report (2:00-3:30)

**[2:00-2:30] THE BACKBONE**
[Text on screen: "THE POLICE REPORT"]
[B-roll: close-up of typed report, redacted document]
"The police report is the backbone of the prosecution's case. It's the arresting officer's narrative of what happened. And it's written by someone whose job is to justify the arrest."

"That's not cynicism. That's the reality of how reports are constructed. The officer is documenting their probable cause for the arrest. They're selecting facts that support the charges."

**[2:30-3:30] WHAT TO LOOK FOR**
[PATTERN INTERRUPT: red highlights appearing on a document graphic]
[Text on screen: "4 things to check in the police report"]
"Four things to check."

"One: the reason for contact. How did the police encounter you? Traffic stop? 911 call? Anonymous tip? The legality of the initial contact determines whether everything that follows is admissible."

"Two: the timeline. Write down every time mentioned. When was the call received? When did the officer arrive? When were you arrested? When were you read Miranda rights? Are there gaps? Do the times even make sense?"

"Three: what you allegedly said. Is it accurate? Is it taken out of context? Is it something you didn't actually say? You're the only person who knows."

"Four: what the officer claims to have observed. Colors, descriptions, locations, actions. If the officer says you were wearing a red shirt and you know you were wearing blue, that's a factual error in the report. Small details like this undermine the officer's reliability as a witness."

---

### Section 3: Witness Statements (3:30-5:00)

**[3:30-4:15] CONSISTENCY AND BIAS**
[Text on screen: "WITNESS STATEMENTS"]
"Witness statements are where cases either solidify or fall apart."

"Look for two things: consistency and bias."

"Consistency: do the witness statements match each other? Do they match the police report? If Officer A says you were aggressive and Officer B says you were cooperative, that's a contradiction the jury should hear."

"Do the statements match the video evidence? If body camera footage exists, compare what the officers wrote with what the camera captured. Discrepancies between written reports and video are some of the most powerful defense tools available."

**[4:15-5:00] WHAT'S MISSING**
[Text on screen: "Who WASN'T interviewed?"]
"Now look at who's missing. Were there witnesses who should have been interviewed but weren't? Bystanders mentioned in the report who never gave statements? People who saw what happened from a different angle?"

"Also: who are the witnesses? Do any of them have a reason to lie? A co-defendant looking for a deal? A confidential informant being paid or receiving leniency? An officer with a disciplinary history?"

"Witness credibility matters. And your attorney should be investigating it."

---

### Section 4: Lab Results and Chain of Custody (5:00-6:30)

**[5:00-5:45] LAB RESULTS**
[PATTERN INTERRUPT: scientific graphics, lab equipment imagery]
[Text on screen: "LAB RESULTS"]
"If your case involves any kind of forensic evidence, drugs, blood alcohol, DNA, fingerprints, the lab results are critical."

"Three things to check. One: does the substance identified match what you're charged with? Charged with amphetamine possession but the lab found MDMA? That's a discrepancy that matters legally."

"Two: was the testing done by a certified lab using accepted methodology? Gas chromatography and mass spectrometry are the gold standards. A field test alone is not confirmation."

"Three: do the weights match? The weight at the scene versus the weight at the lab. In drug cases especially, weight determines everything, the charge level, the mandatory minimum, the sentencing guidelines."

**[5:45-6:30] CHAIN OF CUSTODY**
[Text on screen: "CHAIN OF CUSTODY"]
"Chain of custody is the documentation of every hand the evidence passed through from seizure to courtroom. Every transfer should be logged. Every storage location documented."

"Any gap is a defense argument. If the evidence was weighed at the scene, sat in a patrol car trunk for six hours, and then showed up at the lab lighter than before, where did the missing substance go? That question matters."

"In the case study we'll look at in a minute, the scene weight was 93.9 grams and the lab weight was 25.59 grams. A 73 percent discrepancy. Nobody asked where the other 68 grams went."

---

### Section 5: Video Evidence (6:30-7:30)

**[6:30-7:30] BODY CAMERA AND DASH CAM**
[Text on screen: "VIDEO EVIDENCE"]
"Body camera and dash camera footage is some of the most powerful evidence in any criminal case, for either side."

"Compare the video with the written report. Do the officer's words match what the camera captured? Is the footage complete, or are there gaps? Was the camera turned off at any point during the encounter?"

"Missing footage is itself a fact. If an officer's body camera was on during the traffic stop but turned off during the search, ask why. If dash camera footage that should exist has been lost or corrupted, document that."

"Also look at field sobriety test administration on video. NHTSA has specific protocols. An expert can evaluate whether the officer followed them. Often, they didn't."

### Section 6: Case Study, 500 Pages (7:30-10:00)

**[7:30-8:00] THE SETUP**
[PATTERN INTERRUPT: dramatic pause, screen goes dark, then text fades in]
[Text on screen: "CASE STUDY: 500 PAGES"]
"Here's what happened when one defendant stopped trusting the process and started reading."

"A defendant was facing drug trafficking charges in Florida. Mandatory minimum: three years in state prison. He hired an attorney. Paid thousands. Was told to trust the process. Months passed. No motions filed. Phone calls weren't returned."

"Then one night, he decided to open his own discovery."

**[8:00-8:45] FINDING 1, WEIGHT DISCREPANCY**
[Text on screen: "Finding #1: 73% weight discrepancy"]
"Finding number one. At the scene, officers weighed the substance at 93.9 grams. At the lab, it weighed 25.59 grams. That's 68 grams missing, a 73 percent discrepancy."

"In a trafficking case, weight determines everything. Where did 68 grams go? Packaging separated at the lab? Multiple substances combined at the scene? Chain of custody failure? Each explanation has defense implications. The attorney had raised none of them."

**[8:45-9:15] FINDING 2, PHONE NUMBER MIX-UP**
[Text on screen: "Finding #2: CI phone number matched the defendant's"]
"Finding number two. A confidential informant's phone number listed in the discovery was the same phone number attributed to the defendant elsewhere in the file. The same number couldn't belong to both the informant and the defendant. That's either an error in the investigation or something much more troubling. The attorney hadn't noticed."

**[9:15-9:45] FINDING 3, DRUG TYPE MISMATCH**
[Text on screen: "Finding #3: Charged with amphetamine. Lab found MDMA."]
"Finding number three. The defendant was charged with amphetamine possession. The lab results identified the substance as MDMA and MDA, chemically related but legally distinct compounds with different scheduling and sentencing implications. The charge didn't match the lab result. The attorney hadn't flagged it."

**[9:45-10:00] FINDING 4, FINGERPRINTS**
[Text on screen: "Finding #4: 21 fingerprints. Zero matches."]
"Finding number four. The crime scene report documented 21 latent fingerprints recovered from the location. Zero matched the defendant. Zero. In a case where the prosecution needed to prove possession, not one fingerprint connected the defendant to the evidence. The attorney had filed nothing on any of it."

[Direct to camera, pause]
"This defendant had no legal training. He just read his own paperwork. Four critical issues. All documented in the discovery. All missed by an attorney who was paid to find them."

### Section 7: The Discovery Checklist (10:00-11:30)

**[10:00-10:15] INTRO**
[Text on screen: "THE DISCOVERY CHECKLIST, Screenshot this."]
[Direct to camera, slower pace]
"Here's your discovery reading checklist. Screenshot this. Use it every time you sit down with your file."

**[10:15-11:00] THE CHECKLIST**
[Each item appears on screen as spoken]
"One: Does the police report timeline have gaps or inconsistencies?"
"Two: Do different officers' reports tell the same story?"
"Three: Do witness statements match each other AND the video evidence?"
"Four: Is there body camera or dash camera footage? Is any missing?"
"Five: Do lab results match the charges? Do weights match at scene and lab?"
"Six: Is the chain of custody complete and documented?"
"Seven: Are there witnesses who should have been interviewed but weren't?"
"Eight: Were your statements accurately recorded?"
"Nine: Was the search warrant specific and properly executed?"
"Ten: Are there any Brady materials, evidence favorable to you, that should exist but aren't in the file?"

**[11:00-11:30] WHAT TO DO WITH FINDINGS**
[Text on screen: "How to bring this to your attorney"]
"When you find something, and you will find something, don't just call your attorney and say 'I found a problem.' Write it down. Page number. Document name. What you found. Why it matters. Put it in an email."

"Create a paper trail. If your attorney dismisses what you found without explanation, that response is itself a data point about how they're handling your case."
