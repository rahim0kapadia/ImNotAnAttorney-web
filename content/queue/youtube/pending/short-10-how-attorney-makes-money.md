### Full Script

**[0:00-0:05] HOOK — TEXT ON SCREEN: "Why your attorney's financial incentive flips after you pay"**
[Direct to camera, matter-of-fact tone]
"How your criminal defense attorney gets paid directly shapes how they handle your case. Nobody talks about this. But you need to understand it."

**[0:05-0:16] THE FLIP — TEXT ON SCREEN: "Before you pay: investment. After you pay: cost."**
"Most private attorneys charge a flat retainer — anywhere from five thousand to fifty thousand dollars. Here's what most defendants don't understand: once you pay that retainer, the economic incentive flips. Before you hired them, every hour they spent on you was an investment in landing the engagement. After? Every hour they spend on your case is money they can't bill to a new client."

**[0:16-0:25] THE PRESSURE — TEXT ON SCREEN: "A quick plea is PROFITABLE"**
"This creates a quiet pressure toward early resolution. A quick plea deal is profitable. A three-month suppression battle followed by a trial is expensive. This doesn't mean your attorney will deliberately sell you out. But the pressure toward a plea is baked into the business model."

**[0:25-0:33] PUBLIC DEFENDERS — TEXT ON SCREEN: "Public defenders: different problem, same result"**
"Public defenders? Different problem, same result. Not financial — it's caseload. Three hundred to five hundred cases. Seven minutes per client per week. The math doesn't allow for aggressive defense on every case. Motions don't get filed. Witnesses don't get interviewed. Defendants plead."

**[0:33-0:42] FREE CONSULTATIONS — TEXT ON SCREEN: "Free consultations are SALES calls"**
"And those free consultations? They're sales calls. The attorney is evaluating one thing: can this person pay? The advice is shaped to generate engagement, not to give you an accurate assessment. That doesn't mean every consultation is dishonest. But you should walk in knowing what it is."

**[0:42-0:49] THE QUESTION — TEXT ON SCREEN: "Ask yourself this:"**
"The question to always ask yourself: at every stage of my case, what is the financially optimal outcome for my attorney — and does that match what's optimal for me?"
