Most defendants hire a criminal defense attorney and then go quiet. They wait. They trust. They assume the expensive lawyer is handling everything.

And sometimes they are. But sometimes they're not. And by the time you figure out which, critical deadlines have passed and your options have narrowed to whatever plea the prosecutor feels like offering.

Here are the 10 questions that change that:

1. What is your theory of defense?
2. Have you reviewed all of the discovery?
3. What motions are you planning to file?
4. What does the prosecution have to prove, element by element?
5. What is the worst realistic outcome — and the best?

(Plus 5 more that expose exactly what your attorney has and hasn't done.)

These aren't trick questions. They're what elite defense attorneys wish their clients would ask — because it forces everyone to be on the same page.
