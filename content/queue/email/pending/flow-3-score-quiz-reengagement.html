The 3 biggest factors behind a low score:

1. No motions filed (or you don't know if any were filed)

Motions are the single biggest lever in criminal defense. Suppression motions, motions to compel discovery, motions to dismiss — these are how cases get reduced or thrown out. If your attorney hasn't filed any, or hasn't told you about them, that's a gap worth asking about.

The question: "What motions have you considered filing, and what's your reasoning for filing or not filing each one?"

2. No discovery review with you

Your attorney may have received discovery. They may have even read it. But if they haven't sat down with you and walked through what's in it — the police reports, lab results, witness statements — you're flying blind. Discovery is the prosecution's entire case. You should know what's in it.

The question: "Can we schedule a meeting to review the discovery together? I want to understand what the prosecution has."

3. No strategy conversation

Has your attorney told you their plan? Not "we'll fight it" — the actual strategy. Are they going for suppression? Challenging witnesses? Negotiating a plea? If you can't answer that question, your attorney hasn't answered it either.

The question: "What is our defense strategy, and what are the realistic outcomes you see for my case?"

DUI Cases That Score {{SCORE}}: Here's the Pattern.

You scored {{SCORE}}/100 on the Defense Milestone Score. You're facing DUI charges. Here's what we see in cases like yours.

When DUI defendants score below 50:

The most common gaps are breathalyzer calibration challenges, field sobriety test procedure questions, and DMV hearing deadlines. These aren't exotic defense strategies — they're standard. If your attorney hasn't raised them, it's worth asking why.

The 10-day DMV deadline is the one most defendants miss.

In most states, you have 10 days from arrest to request a DMV hearing to fight license suspension. Miss it and you lose the right to challenge. Period. If your attorney didn't file this within the first week, ask them to explain their reasoning.

Three DUI-specific questions your attorney should be able to answer:

  1. "When was the breathalyzer last calibrated, and have you requested the calibration records?" — The reading is not the case. The maintenance records are.
  2. "Did the arresting officer follow NHTSA standards for the field sobriety tests?" — Officers deviate from the standardized procedure more than you'd think.
  3. "What suppression motions are available based on the stop and the arrest?" — Every DUI starts with a traffic stop. If the stop was bad, everything after it is fruit of the poisonous tree.
