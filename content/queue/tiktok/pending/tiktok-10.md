# TikTok Script #10: Field Sobriety Tests Are Wrong 1 in 3 Times

**Topic:** NHTSA's own data on field sobriety test accuracy
**Format:** talking head + data visualization + text overlay
**Duration:** 30s
**Trending sound suggestion:** Original audio -- voiceover + "wrong answer" game show energy

## Hook (first 3 seconds)
[SECOND 0-1]
**Visual**: Three people standing side by side. Two are highlighted green ("ACTUALLY IMPAIRED"), one is highlighted red ("SOBER -- FALSELY IDENTIFIED").
**Text overlay**: "1 in 3. The one-leg stand is wrong a third of the time."
**Audio**: Game show "wrong answer" sound.

[SECOND 1-3]
**Visual**: Cut to speaker, incredulous expression.
**Audio/Voiceover**: "That's not my number. That's the government's number."

## Body
[SECOND 3-12]
**Visual**: NHTSA accuracy data appearing as a bar chart.
**Audio/Voiceover**: "NHTSA -- the agency that created field sobriety tests -- published their own accuracy rates. The HGN eye test: 77%. Walk-and-turn: 68%. One-leg stand: 65%. All three combined, under perfect conditions: 82%. That means 18% of the time, even done perfectly, a sober person gets flagged as impaired."
**Text overlay**: "HGN: 77%. Walk-and-turn: 68%. One-leg stand: 65%. Combined: 82%."

[SECOND 12-20]
**Visual**: Officer on the side of a highway at night. Uneven pavement. Car headlights glaring.
**Audio/Voiceover**: "Those numbers assume perfect administration. Perfect conditions. Perfect scoring. On a flat, dry, well-lit surface. How often does that happen on the shoulder of a highway at midnight with semis blowing past?"
**Text overlay**: "Perfect conditions? On a highway. At midnight. In traffic."

[SECOND 20-27]
**Visual**: Text list of common officer errors.
**Audio/Voiceover**: "Officers rush the eye test. They give walk-and-turn on gravel or slopes. They don't ask about medical conditions that mimic impairment -- inner ear problems, knee injuries, neurological conditions. They don't offer you the chance to remove high heels. If they deviated from NHTSA standards, the results aren't standardized tests. They're subjective opinions."
**Text overlay**: "ASK: Were NHTSA standards followed exactly? On what surface? Was I asked about medical conditions?"

[SECOND 27-30]
**Visual**: The three people from the opening -- but now the "sober" person has a speech bubble: "Ask your attorney about the administration standards."
**Text overlay**: "Their own data says it's wrong 18% of the time. imnotanattorney.com"
**Audio**: Clean resolution.
