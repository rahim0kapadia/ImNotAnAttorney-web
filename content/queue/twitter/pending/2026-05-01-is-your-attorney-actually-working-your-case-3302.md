You hired a criminal defense attorney. You paid thousands.

And now: silence.

"We're working on it" is not an update. Here's how to tell if your attorney is actually working your case — or just cashing the retainer:
===TWEET===
First, know what "working" actually looks like.
===TWEET===
In the first 2 weeks, your attorney should have:
✓ Obtained or demanded discovery
✓ Reviewed the charging documents
✓ Had a strategy conversation with you
✓ Entered an appearance with the court

If none of this has happened, ask why.
===TWEET===
By month one:
✓ Thoroughly reviewed all discovery
✓ Identified suppression issues (was the stop legal? search valid? Miranda respected?)
✓ Communicated a preliminary strategy
✓ Filed any time-sensitive motions (some deadlines are early and missing them is malpractice)
===TWEET===
Red flag #1: You can't get a callback within 48 hours.

Not every call, every time. But consistently? Something is wrong.

Document your attempts. Write: "I've called on [dates] with no response. Please update me by [date]."

That paper trail matters.
===TWEET===
Red flag #2: They can't tell you what motions they've filed.

Every criminal case has potential motions. Suppression. Discovery. Dismiss. If your attorney has filed zero and can't explain why — they may not be working your case.
===TWEET===
Red flag #3: They push the plea deal without explaining alternatives.

"Just take the deal" is not legal strategy. It's laziness.

A good attorney presents options. A bad one pressures you toward what's easier for them.
