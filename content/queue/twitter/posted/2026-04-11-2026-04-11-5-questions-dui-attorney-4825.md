Most DUI defendants pay $5,000–$15,000 and never ask a single hard question.

The attorney says "we'll handle it."

Here are 5 questions that actually make them prove it:
===TWEET===
1/ "Was the traffic stop legal? "

Officers need ARTICULABLE FACTS to pull you over. Not a hunch. Not "driving slowly. "

If the stop was illegal, everything that followed — field sobriety, breathalyzer, arrest — can be suppressed.
===TWEET===
Your attorney should have answered this in week one.
===TWEET===
2/ "Have you reviewed the breathalyzer calibration records?"

Breathalyzers require regular calibration. If the machine was out of spec, that number they keep waving around might not hold up.

Someone has to actually request those records. Has your attorney?
===TWEET===
3/ "What motions have you filed — or plan to file?"

Motion to suppress. Motion to dismiss. Motion to compel discovery.

If the answer is "none" or "we'll see" — that's not a strategy. That's passive representation of someone who's already given up.
===TWEET===
4/ "Can I see the dashcam and bodycam footage?"

You have the right to see the evidence against you.

If your attorney hasn't requested it — or won't show it to you — you need to know why.

The footage often tells a different story than the police report.
===TWEET===
5/ "What's your actual strategy for my case? "

"Wait for the plea offer" is not a strategy. A real DUI defense examines: the stop, the field sobriety tests, the chemical tests, the officer's training records.
===TWEET===
If your attorney can't articulate a plan, you deserve to know that now.
===TWEET===
