"Can my charges be dropped?"

Yes. But probably not the way you think.

Charges don't drop because you're a good person or said sorry. They drop for specific, technical, legal reasons. Here's what those actually are:
===TWEET===
Reason 1: Insufficient evidence

The prosecution reviews their case and realizes they can't prove it beyond a reasonable doubt.
===TWEET===
What your attorney should be doing: Identifying every weakness in the prosecution's evidence and communicating those weaknesses directly to the prosecutor. Sometimes a well-drafted motion or pointed letter is enough.
===TWEET===
Reason 2: Constitutional violations

Illegal search? Unlawful stop? Miranda violation? If the evidence was obtained through a constitutional violation, a motion to suppress can get it excluded.
===TWEET===
Without that evidence, the prosecution may not have enough to proceed — and the charges get dropped.
===TWEET===
What does NOT get charges dropped:

✗ "It was my first offense" (affects sentencing, not charges)
✗ "The victim doesn't want to press charges" (the STATE brings charges, not the victim — this is a TV myth)
✗ "I said I was sorry" (can actually be used as evidence of guilt)

The stage matters:

Charges can be dropped at any point:
• Before filing (prosecutor declines to charge)
• Pre-trial (motions, prosecutorial discretion, diversion programs)
• During trial (directed verdict, prosecution drops mid-trial)

The earlier the better.
===TWEET===
Once a case goes to trial, dismissal becomes much harder.
