## Thread (7 tweets)

**Tweet 1 — Hook (no link, no hashtags)**
After a DUI arrest, a 10-day clock starts.

Miss it and your license gets automatically suspended.

No hearing. No appeal. No second chance.

Most attorneys don't mention this until it's too late.

---

**Tweet 2**
This is completely separate from your criminal case.

Your criminal attorney is focused on the charges, the plea, the court dates.

Meanwhile your license is on a countdown clock that nobody mentioned.

Two systems. One arrest. Different deadlines.

---

**Tweet 3**
What most attorneys do with the DMV hearing: file the paperwork to preserve your license, show up, lose, move on.

What the attorneys who actually understand DUI defense do: use it as a free deposition of the arresting officer.

Under oath. Before trial. Months earlier.

---

**Tweet 4**
The officer has to testify at the DMV hearing. Your attorney gets to cross-examine them.

Every answer the officer gives is locked in.

If they testify differently at trial months later — that inconsistency is ammunition.

It's a preview of the prosecution's case before they show their cards.

---

**Tweet 5**
5 questions to ask your DUI attorney this week:

- Has the DMV hearing been requested?
- Are you attending and cross-examining — not just filing paperwork?
- What's your strategy for the hearing?
- Will you preserve the transcript?
- What happens to my license between now and the hearing?

---

**Tweet 6**
If your attorney hasn't mentioned the DMV hearing — or mentioned it only to say "we'll file the paperwork" — that clock doesn't pause while you wait for a callback.

A 4-month license suspension in California. 6 months in Florida. All for missing a 10-day window.
