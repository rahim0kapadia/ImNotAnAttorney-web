The quality of your questions determines the quality of your defense.

Most defendants hire an attorney and go quiet. They wait. They trust.

Here are 10 questions that force your attorney to prove they're working your case:
===TWEET===
Q1: "What is your theory of defense?"

The most important question you'll ever ask. And most defendants never ask it.

Good answer: "We're arguing the stop was illegal because..."
Bad answer: "We'll see what they offer."

If they don't have a theory, they don't have a defense.
===TWEET===
Q2: "Have you reviewed all of the discovery?"

Discovery = every piece of evidence against you. Your attorney should have read every page, watched every video.

Follow up: "Can I see it?" and "What did you find that helps us?"

You have the right to this information.
===TWEET===
Q3: "What motions are you planning to file?"

Motion to suppress. Brady motion. Motion to compel. Motion to dismiss.

If your attorney says "we don't need any motions" — ask them to explain specifically why each one doesn't apply.

Specifics matter. Vague answers don't.
===TWEET===
Q4: "What element of the prosecution's case is weakest?"

Every charge has elements. Every element must be proven beyond a reasonable doubt.

If the prosecution can't prove even ONE element, they can't convict.

Your whole defense might live in that one weak element.
===TWEET===
Q8: "What are the weaknesses in the prosecution's case?"

Every case has weaknesses. Every single one.

If your attorney says "they have a strong case" and leaves it there — push back. An attorney who can't identify a single weakness hasn't looked hard enough. Or has already given up.
