Nobody gives you a map when you get charged with a crime.

Here's the one you should have gotten:

9 stages. Where cases are actually won and lost. And what you should be doing at each one.
===TWEET===
Stage 1-3: Arrest → Bond → Arraignment

• You're taken into custody and processed (hours)
• Bond hearing sets conditions for release (within 72 hours)
• Arraignment: you enter a plea — almost always "not guilty" — and get a next court date (5-15 minutes)

===TWEET===
It's a procedural checkpoint. Real work begins after.
===TWEET===
Stage 4-5: Discovery + Motions

This is where cases are actually won. Discovery = every piece of evidence the prosecution has. Gaps, inconsistencies, constitutional violations — they live here. Motions = your attorney's primary weapon.
===TWEET===
A successful motion to suppress can gut the prosecution's case before trial ever starts. These two stages receive the least attention from passive attorneys and the most attention from great ones.
===TWEET===
Stage 6: Plea Negotiations

In the federal system, 97% of convictions come from guilty pleas — not trials. In state systems: 90-95%. For most defendants, the plea negotiation IS the case.
===TWEET===
What determines the outcome: motion success, prosecution caseload, your attorney's trial credibility, and whether the prosecution believes you're actually prepared to go to trial.
===TWEET===
The trial penalty — what the system doesn't advertise:

Defendants who exercise their right to trial and lose often receive sentences 3-6x longer than those who accepted a plea. This isn't misconduct. It's a documented feature of the system.
===TWEET===
Understanding this pressure is part of making an informed decision about whether to accept a plea.
