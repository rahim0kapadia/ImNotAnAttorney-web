You haven't heard from your attorney in six weeks.

Is that normal?

Here's the gap between what's legally required, what's professionally expected, and what most defendants actually experience:
===TWEET===
What the bar actually requires (ABA Model Rule 1.
===TWEET===
4):

✓ Promptly inform you of decisions requiring your consent
✓ Keep you reasonably informed about your case status
✓ Promptly respond to reasonable information requests
✓ Explain things to the extent needed for you to make informed decisions

Notice: "reasonably" appears every…
===TWEET===
That word is doing a lot of work.
===TWEET===
What good attorneys actually do (minimum standards):

| Event | When you should hear from them |
|-------|-------------------------------|
| Discovery received | Within 2 weeks |
| Motion filed or ruled on | Within 24 hours |
| Plea offer received | Immediately |
| Court date | …
===TWEET===
What most defendants actually experience:

"I paid $15,000 and haven't spoken to my attorney in 2 months. "

"The only time I hear from them is when I call — and even then it takes a week. "

"I found out about my court date from the court's automated system.
===TWEET===
"

These aren't outliers. This is the norm for an alarming number of defendants.
===TWEET===
How to get real answers (without sounding aggressive):

Don't ask: "What's the status of my case? " (easy to dodge)

Ask specifically:
• "Have you received all discovery? "
• "Have any motions been filed? "
• "Has there been any communication with the prosecution?
===TWEET===
"

Specific questions require specific answers. Vague ones don't.
