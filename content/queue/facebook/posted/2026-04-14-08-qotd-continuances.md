### Post Content

How many continuances has your attorney requested in your case?

One is normal. Two might be strategic. Three starts to raise questions. Four or more?

Ask this question: "What specific work did you complete between the last court date and now?"

Because a continuance is supposed to mean "I need more time to prepare." It is NOT supposed to mean "I need more time because I haven't started."

Continuances can be genuinely strategic:
- Waiting for lab results
- Scheduling a key expert witness
- Allowing time for a related ruling that could help your case
- Completing an investigation

But continuances can also be scheduling management — your attorney has too many cases and they're pushing yours back to make room for others.

The way you tell the difference: ask what changed between the last continuance and this one. A strategic continuance comes with a specific answer. A scheduling continuance gets you a vague one.

How many continuances are you at? What reason were you given?

(This is information, not legal advice.)
