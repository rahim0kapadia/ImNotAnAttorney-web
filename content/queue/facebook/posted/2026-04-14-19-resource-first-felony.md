### Post Content

A felony charge is not a felony conviction.

Read that again.

According to the Bureau of Justice Statistics, approximately 33% of felony defendants in state courts are not convicted. That includes dismissals, acquittals, and diversions.

Of those who ARE convicted, many receive probation rather than incarceration, especially first-time offenders.

If you or someone you love is staring at the word FELONY on a piece of paper right now, here is what you need to know:

THE TIMELINE:
- Arrest to initial appearance: 24-48 hours
- Arraignment: 2-4 weeks
- Discovery received: 2-8 weeks
- Pre-trial motions: 4-12 weeks
- Plea negotiations: Months 2-6+
- Trial (if needed): 6-12+ months

OPTIONS FOR FIRST-TIME OFFENDERS:
- Diversion programs (complete a program, charges dismissed, record stays clean)
- Deferred adjudication (plead guilty, conviction not entered if you complete probation)
- Charge reduction (felony reduced to misdemeanor)
- Withhold of adjudication (found guilty but conviction doesn't go on record, some states)

THE 5 BIGGEST MISTAKES:
1. Talking to police without an attorney present
2. Not reading your own discovery
3. Accepting the first plea offer without investigation
4. Assuming the worst before the process even starts
5. Being a passenger in your own defense instead of asking questions

A felony charge is the beginning of a process. The more you understand that process, the better your outcome will be.

(This is information, not legal advice. Consult with a licensed attorney in your jurisdiction.)
