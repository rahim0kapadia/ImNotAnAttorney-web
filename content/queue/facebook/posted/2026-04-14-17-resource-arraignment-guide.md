### Post Content

Your first court date is coming up and you don't know what's going to happen. You've never been inside a courtroom before. You don't know where to stand, what to say, or if you're going to jail.

Here is the truth: your arraignment is probably the least dramatic event in your entire case. But knowing what to expect makes it a lot less terrifying.

What actually happens, step by step:

1. You arrive 30 minutes early. Dress like a job interview.
2. You check in with the clerk. You sit down. You wait. Possibly for hours.
3. Your name is called. You walk to the front. Your attorney should be with you.
4. The charges are read. Listen carefully, make sure they match what you were arrested for.
5. You enter a plea. Your attorney will almost certainly say "not guilty." This is standard. It does NOT mean you're lying, it means you're preserving your rights.
6. Bail conditions are discussed or confirmed.
7. The next court date is set. Write it down.
8. You leave.

The whole thing takes about 5 minutes once your name is called.

The biggest mistake defendants make at arraignment: talking to police, prosecutors, or co-defendants about their case. Say nothing to anyone except your attorney. The only words you need: "I'd like to speak with my attorney."

What to ask your attorney AFTER arraignment:
- "When will you receive discovery?"
- "What's our initial strategy?"
- "Are there any immediate deadlines?"
- "When should I expect to hear from you next?"

That last one is important. Set the expectation now, not after months of silence.

(This is information, not legal advice. Court procedures vary by state.)
