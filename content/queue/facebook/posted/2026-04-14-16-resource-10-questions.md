### Post Content

If you take nothing else from this post, take these 10 questions to your next meeting with your attorney.

Write them down. Bring a pen. Write down the answers.

1. Have you received and reviewed ALL discovery?
2. What are the three biggest weaknesses in the prosecution's case?
3. What motions have you filed or considered filing?
4. What is my realistic sentence exposure — not the maximum, the realistic range?
5. What are the collateral consequences if I plead guilty? (job, housing, custody, immigration)
6. Is there a diversion program or deferred adjudication option?
7. Have you investigated the prosecution's witnesses?
8. If I take this plea, what exactly am I admitting to?
9. Can we counter-offer with different terms?
10. What would you do if this were your family member's case?

These are not trick questions. They are the questions that good attorneys expect from engaged clients. An attorney who takes them seriously is an attorney who is working your case. An attorney who gets defensive or dismissive is telling you something about their effort level.

The defendants who ask hard questions early get better outcomes than the ones who stay quiet and hope for the best.

Print this. Screenshot this. Bring it to court.
