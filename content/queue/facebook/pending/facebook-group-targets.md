# Facebook Group Target List for ImNotAnAttorney

## Audience on Facebook

Facebook skews older than TikTok/Instagram. The INNA audience here is primarily:
- Parents, spouses, and family members of defendants (researching on behalf of their loved ones)
- Defendants aged 30-55 who use Facebook as their primary social platform
- People post-arrest in crisis mode searching for support and information
- Criminal justice reform advocates who amplify educational content

**Core insight:** On Facebook, the person reading the content is often NOT the defendant. It is the mother, the wife, the partner doing research at 2 AM. Write for them.

---

## TIER 1: Highest-Value Groups (largest, most active, best audience fit)

### 1. Strong Prison Wives
- **URL:** facebook.com/groups/StrongPrisonWives
- **Estimated size:** 50,000+ members
- **Focus:** Wives and partners of incarcerated individuals
- **Posting rules:** No solicitation, no negativity, support-focused
- **Content that works:** Story posts about families navigating the system. Posts framed as "how to help your partner from the outside." Question-to-ask-your-attorney content resonates because these women are the ones making calls and attending meetings on behalf of their partners.
- **Entry strategy:** Respond to existing posts with genuine empathy and information for 2 weeks before posting original content.

### 2. Incarcerated Loved Ones
- **URL:** Search "Incarcerated Loved Ones" on Facebook (16,500+ members per academic research, PMC, SAGE journals)
- **Estimated size:** 16,500+ members (verified via academic study)
- **Focus:** Support, advocacy, and information sharing for families of incarcerated people
- **Posting rules:** Encouragement-focused, no judgment, information sharing encouraged
- **Content that works:** "Know Your Rights" content. Discovery rights, attorney communication standards, bar complaints. This group actively welcomes informational posts. Members share experiences and seek connection (confirmed by 2025 SAGE research on this specific group).
- **Entry strategy:** The group culture is information-forward. Educational content is welcomed early. Start with colored text background posts with single facts.

### 3. Inmate Support Loved Ones Of The Incarcerated
- **URL:** facebook.com/groups/842620995799311
- **Estimated size:** 10,000-20,000 members
- **Focus:** Emotional support and practical guidance for families
- **Posting rules:** Support-focused, respectful
- **Content that works:** Family-centric angles: "What family members can do from the outside," "Questions to bring to the attorney meeting," "How to request the case file." Story posts about mothers and partners doing research hit hard here.
- **Entry strategy:** Lead with empathy. Respond to people in distress first. Establish presence through comments before original posts.

### 4. Women with Incarcerated Partners
- **URL:** Search "women with incarcerated partners" on Facebook
- **Estimated size:** 10,000-30,000 members
- **Focus:** Women navigating relationships with incarcerated men
- **Posting rules:** Women-only typically, support-focused, no judgment
- **Content that works:** "How to help your partner hold their lawyer accountable." Content about requesting case files, understanding discovery, and filing bar complaints on behalf of a loved one. These women are often the de facto case managers.
- **Entry strategy:** Be a helpful, consistent presence. Never pitch. Let your profile link do the work.

### 5. Families Against Mandatory Minimums (FAMM) Community
- **URL:** Search "FAMM" or "Families Against Mandatory Minimums" on Facebook
- **Estimated size:** 30,000+ followers/members across pages and groups
- **Focus:** Advocacy for sentencing reform, family support
- **Posting rules:** Advocacy-aligned, factual, no personal attacks
- **Content that works:** Data-driven posts about plea deal statistics, sentencing exposure, the economics of criminal defense. This audience is politically engaged and responds to evidence-backed content. The 97% federal plea rate statistic performs well here.
- **Entry strategy:** Share systemic insights, not individual case advice. This audience values data and context.

## TIER 2: High-Value Groups (strong audience fit, active communities)

### 6. DUI/DWI Support Groups (multiple groups, search broadly)
- **URL:** Search "DUI Support Group" or "DWI Support" on Facebook
- **Estimated size:** 5,000-15,000 per group (multiple exist)
- **Focus:** First-time DUI defendants, repeat offenders, families
- **Posting rules:** No judgment, support-focused, practical information
- **Content that works:** DUI is INNA's deepest content area. Breathalyzer calibration, field sobriety test standards, the 10-day DMV deadline, can-DUI-be-dismissed angles. Question posts like "Has your attorney filed a motion to suppress the traffic stop?" generate massive engagement. These groups are desperate for non-judgmental, practical information.
- **CAUTION:** DUI defendants should limit social media activity per legal advice. Frame content as informational, never discuss specific cases. Remind people to consult their attorney.

### 7. Drug Charge Support and Information Groups
- **URL:** Search "drug charge support" or "drug offense help" on Facebook
- **Estimated size:** 3,000-12,000 per group
- **Focus:** People facing drug charges, families of drug offenders
- **Posting rules:** No judgment, no glorification, support and information
- **Content that works:** Constructive possession, field test vs. lab test, chain of custody. These are technical details that can change outcomes and most defendants never hear about them. The field test false positive angle is the single highest-engagement topic for drug defense content.
- **Entry strategy:** Lead with facts, not opinions. Technical content builds trust with this audience.

### 8. Federal Defendant Support Groups
- **URL:** Search "federal charges support" or "federal defendant" on Facebook
- **Estimated size:** 2,000-8,000 members
- **Focus:** Federal cases with longer timelines and different procedures
- **Posting rules:** Support-focused, information sharing
- **Content that works:** Federal discovery rules, the 97% federal plea rate, cooperation agreements, wire fraud defense questions. Federal cases create extreme anxiety due to longer sentences and the perception that "the feds always win." Content that provides perspective and action steps resonates.
- **Entry strategy:** Federal families are highly engaged and read carefully. Longer posts work here.

### 9. Criminal Justice Reform Network
- **URL:** Search "Criminal Justice Reform" on Facebook (multiple groups)
- **Estimated size:** 10,000-50,000 across related groups
- **Focus:** Policy reform, advocacy, storytelling
- **Posting rules:** Factual, respectful debate encouraged
- **Content that works:** Public defender caseload data, plea deal statistics, how attorney economics create misaligned incentives. Longer posts with citations. This audience reads everything and comments thoughtfully.
- **Entry strategy:** Lead with data and systemic analysis. This audience rejects anything that feels like marketing.

### 10. Know Your Rights Community Groups (state-specific)
- **URL:** Search "Know Your Rights [state name]" on Facebook
- **Estimated size:** 1,000-20,000 per state group
- **Focus:** Legal education, civil rights, police encounters
- **Posting rules:** Educational focus, no legal advice, no solicitation
- **Content that works:** "Did you know?" format. Discovery rights, Miranda rights, attorney deadline obligations. These groups specifically want the type of educational content INNA creates. The overlap is almost perfect.
- **Entry strategy:** Educational content is welcome from day one. Match the group's existing format.

## Engagement Calendar

**Week 1-2:** Pure value only. Respond to other people's posts with genuine empathy and helpful information. Build recognition. Zero self-promotion. Zero links. Zero mentions of INNA.

**Week 3-4:** Start posting original content (Know Your Rights facts, Question posts). No links. No product mentions. Just value.

**Week 5-6:** Begin adding blog links in first comments on your own posts. Profile should link to imnotanattorney.com. Use the first-comment method only.

**Week 7+:** Consistent posting schedule. Mix of all content types. Respond to every comment on your posts. Build reputation as the "person who actually knows about defendant rights."

## Rules of Engagement

1. NEVER sell in groups. No product mentions. No pricing. No "check out our service." Ever.
2. NEVER give legal advice. Always frame as "questions to consider asking your attorney."
3. ALWAYS include the disclaimer: "This is information, not legal advice."
4. Respond to people in crisis with genuine empathy FIRST, information SECOND.
5. If someone asks "what should I do?", respond with "here are questions you might want to ask your attorney," NEVER "you should do X."
6. Build relationships with group admins. Be their most helpful, consistent contributor.
7. Track which groups drive profile visits and website traffic via UTM parameters on your profile link.
8. Post to one group per day per piece of content. Do NOT cross-post the same content to 5 groups in one day, that looks like spam and gets flagged.
9. Vary content type by group culture: data-heavy for reform groups, story-heavy for family groups, how-to-heavy for DUI groups.
10. If a post gets removed by admins, do NOT repost. Message the admin, ask what was wrong, adjust, and try a different piece next time.
