### Post Content

Something happens the moment someone gets arrested. The power dynamic flips.

Before the arrest, you are a person who makes decisions about your own life. After the arrest, suddenly everyone else is making decisions for you. The officer. The judge. The prosecutor. And then, the person you hired to help you.

Here is what nobody tells defendants: your attorney works for YOU. You are the client. They are the service provider. You hired them, or the court assigned them to represent YOUR interests.

That means:

YOU decide whether to accept or reject a plea deal. Not them. They advise. You decide.

YOU decide whether to testify at trial. Not them. They advise. You decide.

YOU have the right to be informed about every development in your case.

YOU have the right to a copy of your case file.

YOU have the right to ask questions and receive answers that make sense to you, not legal jargon designed to end the conversation.

YOU have the right to fire your attorney if they are not performing.

The criminal justice system is intimidating by design. It makes defendants feel small, confused, and dependent. But the attorney-client relationship is not supposed to work that way.

You are not a passenger in your own defense. You are the decision-maker.

Ask questions. Demand answers. Document everything. That is not being difficult. That is being responsible.

(This is information, not legal advice.)

### UPL Compliance Note

This post states factual rights that exist in the attorney-client relationship. It does not advise anyone to take specific legal action. The rights described (right to information, right to make plea decisions, right to case file, right to terminate representation) are universally established.
