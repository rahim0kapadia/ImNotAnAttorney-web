## Facebook Post: What Do You Wish You'd Known?

**Type**: Text post (colored background)
**Distribution**: Groups (rotate across 3-4 groups, one per day)
**Suggested time**: Thursday 7:00 PM ET
**Format**: Colored text background (deep navy)

---

### Post Content

What is one thing you wish someone had told you before your first court date?

Not legal advice. Not what your attorney said. The thing a friend or family member could have told you that would have made that day less terrifying.

I'll go first:

I wish someone had told me that the arraignment itself takes about five minutes. I spent three days convinced I was walking into a trial. I didn't sleep. I didn't eat. I rehearsed what I was going to say to the judge.

Then my name got called, the charges were read, my attorney said "not guilty," and we walked out. The whole thing was over before I understood what happened.

All that panic for five minutes.

What's yours?

(This is information, not legal advice.)

### Visual Direction

Colored text background, deep navy or dark blue-black. The question format with a personal-feeling opening creates the illusion of a conversation, not a brand post. No image needed. The colored background forces brevity and visual standout in the feed.

### Link Strategy

No link. Pure engagement post. The goal is generating a long comment thread of personal experiences. If someone describes a situation where specific blog content would help, reply with the relevant blog link in a comment.

### Engagement Strategy

Reply to every comment with genuine acknowledgment: "That's a huge one" or "I hear that, nobody talks about that part." Ask follow-up questions to keep threads going. Do NOT redirect to products. Let the thread build organically. The algorithm rewards posts with multi-comment threads.

### UPL Compliance Note

This post asks for personal reflections, not legal questions. If someone turns their comment into a legal question ("should I plead guilty?"), redirect: "That's really a question for your attorney. But here's something to consider asking them: [relevant question from INNA blog content]."
