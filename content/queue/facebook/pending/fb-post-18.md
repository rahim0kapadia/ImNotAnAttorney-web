### Post Content

If you are facing a DUI charge, there is a checklist of things your attorney should be doing. Most defendants do not know what this checklist looks like -- so they have no way to know if the work is getting done.

Here is what a thorough DUI defense involves:

THE STOP:
- Was there reasonable suspicion for the traffic stop?
- Is there dashcam footage of the stop? Does it match the police report?
- What was the stated reason for the stop, and does the evidence support it?

THE FIELD SOBRIETY TESTS:
- Were the tests administered according to NHTSA standards?
- What were the conditions? Flat surface? Adequate lighting? Weather?
- Was the officer certified to administer the tests?
- Were the results recorded accurately?

THE BREATH TEST:
- Was the breathalyzer device properly calibrated?
- When was it last calibrated? Is there a calibration record?
- Was the 15-20 minute observation period followed before the test?
- Did anything happen during the observation period (burping, vomiting, use of mouthwash) that could affect the result?

THE BLOOD TEST (if applicable):
- Was the draw conducted by a qualified technician?
- Was the chain of custody maintained?
- Were the samples stored properly?

THE DMV HEARING:
- In many states, you have a LIMITED window (often 10 days) to request a DMV hearing to challenge license suspension. If your attorney missed this deadline, ask why.

If your attorney has not addressed each of these areas -- or cannot explain why a particular area is not relevant to your case -- the defense has not been fully developed.

This is not legal advice. This is a checklist of questions that informed clients bring to their DUI attorney meetings. Every one of these areas represents a potential challenge to the prosecution's case.
