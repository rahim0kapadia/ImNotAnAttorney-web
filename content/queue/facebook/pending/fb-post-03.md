## Facebook Post: What Did They Say About Strategy?

**Type**: Text post (colored background)
**Distribution**: Groups (rotate across 3-4 groups, one per day)
**Suggested time**: Saturday 7:30 PM ET
**Format**: Colored text background (dark purple)

---

### Post Content

I want to hear something real from this group.

The next time you talk to your attorney -- or if you already have recently -- ask them this one question:

"What is your theory of defense in my case?"

Then tell me what they said. Word for word.

Because there is a huge difference between:

"The traffic stop lacked reasonable suspicion because the officer's stated reason contradicts the dashcam footage, so we are filing a motion to suppress everything found after the stop."

and

"Don't worry about it. I've got this."

One of those is a defense strategy. The other one is a sentence designed to end the conversation.

If your attorney has a real strategy, they can explain it in plain language. They can tell you WHAT they plan to challenge, WHY they plan to challenge it, and WHAT the potential outcome is.

If they can't -- or won't -- that tells you something.

What did yours say?

(This is information, not legal advice.)

### Visual Direction

Colored text background -- dark purple. The side-by-side comparison (specific strategy vs. vague dismissal) is the core of this post. No image needed.

### Link Strategy

No link. Engagement only. If someone describes a vague non-answer from their attorney, respond with empathy and suggest they ask a more specific version of the question.

### Engagement Strategy

This post will generate three types of responses: (1) people with articulate attorneys who will share what they heard (valuable social proof), (2) people who got dismissed or ignored (validates their frustration), and (3) people who realize they have never asked. Engage all three. For group 3, encourage them to ask this week and come back to share.

### UPL Compliance Note

Do NOT evaluate anyone's attorney's strategy in the comments. If someone shares what their attorney said and asks "is that good?" respond with: "I can't evaluate that -- that's between you and your attorney. But the fact that they could articulate a specific approach is a positive sign." Or if it was vague: "If you want more detail, try asking: 'What specific evidence are you planning to challenge and how?'"
