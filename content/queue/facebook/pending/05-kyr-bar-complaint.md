### Post Content

Your attorney works for you. If they are not doing their job, there is a formal process to hold them accountable.

It is called a bar complaint.

Every state has a disciplinary body that investigates attorneys who violate their ethical obligations. You can file a complaint. It goes on their record. They are required to respond. And in serious cases, they can be suspended or lose their license.

What qualifies:
- Abandonment (they stopped communicating and stopped working your case)
- Missed deadlines that harmed your case
- Lying about the status of your case or what they filed
- Mishandling your retainer funds
- Conflicts of interest
- Agreeing to terms without your consent

What happens when you file:
1. The state bar reviews your complaint
2. They notify the attorney, who MUST respond in writing
3. An investigation occurs if warranted
4. Outcomes range from private reprimand to disbarment

Here is the part nobody tells you: even complaints that get dismissed still create a record. If three different clients file complaints about the same attorney, a pattern emerges. Your complaint might be the one that tips the scale.

And many attorneys who receive a bar complaint suddenly become very responsive. Not because they had a change of heart — because their license is now on the line.

To file: Search "[your state] bar complaint" and follow the process. Gather your documentation first: retainer agreement, communication records, court docket showing what was (and was not) filed, timeline of events.

Document everything. It matters more than you think.

(This is information, not legal advice.)
