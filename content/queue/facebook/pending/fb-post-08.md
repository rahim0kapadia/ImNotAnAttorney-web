## Facebook Post: The First Offer Is Not the Best Offer

### Post Content

The prosecution's first plea offer is almost never their best offer.

Read that again.

When a prosecutor puts a deal on the table, they are not doing you a favor. They are opening a negotiation. Just like a car dealer does not start at their lowest price.

"Take this before it gets worse."
"This offer expires Friday."
"You really don't want to go to trial."

These are negotiation tactics. They might sound like legal wisdom, but they are pressure, designed to close the case fast.

Here is what changes the negotiation:

- Discovery review. When your attorney has read every page and found inconsistencies, the prosecution knows they have a fight on their hands.
- Motion filing. A suppression motion, even one that gets denied, tells the prosecution you are not going quietly.
- Witness investigation. When the defense has looked into the credibility of the State's witnesses, the dynamic shifts.

The prosecution makes their best offers when they believe going to trial will cost them time, money, and uncertainty. If your attorney has not done any of the above, the prosecution has no reason to improve the offer.

A plea offer that comes before investigation is a guess. A plea offer that comes after investigation is an informed negotiation.

There IS a time when the first offer is the right move. When your attorney has done the work, reviewed the evidence, filed relevant motions, and then says: "This is a strong deal given the circumstances." That is earned advice. That is different from "just take it."

The question to ask: "What work have you done that led you to recommend this plea?"

(This is information, not legal advice.)

### Engagement Strategy

This post will generate strong reactions from people who accepted first offers and regretted it, and from people currently being pressured. Respond with empathy. For people currently being pressured: "You have the right to take the time you need. Ask your attorney what work has been done to evaluate this offer before you decide."

### UPL Compliance Note

Do NOT tell anyone to reject their plea offer. Do NOT say first offers are always bad. Note explicitly in the post (as written above) that there are times when the first offer is the right move, when the attorney has done the work. Frame it as: make an informed decision, not a pressured one.
