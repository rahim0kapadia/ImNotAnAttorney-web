### Post Content

Most attorney meetings go like this:

"Anything new?"
"Not really. We're waiting on a few things."
"Okay."
"Okay."
"See you next time."

Five minutes. Zero information. Zero accountability.

Here are 5 questions that change the conversation:

1. "What specific work have you completed on my case since we last spoke?"

Not "are you working on it?" WHAT have you done? Specifically.

2. "Is all discovery in? Have you reviewed every page?"

Not a general sense. Have you physically read the police reports, the lab results, the witness statements?

3. "What are the three biggest weaknesses in the prosecution's case?"

If they cannot name weaknesses, they have not done the analysis.

4. "What motions have you filed or are you planning to file?"

Motions are how your attorney challenges the evidence. No motions means no challenge.

5. "What is the next concrete step in my defense, and when will it happen?"

Not "we'll see." A specific action with a specific timeline.

Write these down. Bring them to your next meeting. Take notes on the answers.

An attorney who takes these questions seriously is an attorney who respects your engagement. An attorney who gets defensive is telling you something about their work product.

(This is information, not legal advice.)
