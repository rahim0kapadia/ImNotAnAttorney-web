## Quora: INNA-Q06 | I got my first felony charge. Am I going to prison?
**Topics**: Felony Charges, First Offense, Criminal Defense, Sentencing, Criminal Justice
**SEO**: "first felony charge what happens" / "first time felon prison" / "will I go to jail first offense"

Take a breath. What you're imagining and what actually happens are probably very different.

According to the Bureau of Justice Statistics, approximately 33% of felony defendants in state courts are not convicted. That includes dismissals, acquittals, and diversions. Of those who ARE convicted, many receive probation rather than incarceration -- especially first-time offenders.

A felony charge does not mean a felony conviction. And a felony conviction does not automatically mean prison.

**What actually happens, stage by stage:**

**The arrest (Days 1-3).** You're brought before a judge within 24-48 hours. The single most important rule: do NOT talk to police without an attorney present. This isn't a TV cliche -- it's the rule that defendants break most often and regret most deeply. Also don't discuss your case with anyone in holding. Other inmates can and do testify.

**Discovery (Weeks 1-8).** The prosecution must turn over all evidence. Buried in those hundreds of pages are the details that can save you. Do police reports from different officers tell the same story? Is there body camera footage, and does it match the written reports? Are there gaps in the chain of custody?

**Pre-trial motions (Weeks 4-12).** Your attorney files motions -- formal requests to suppress illegally obtained evidence, dismiss charges, or compel the prosecution to turn over withheld information. This is where many cases are actually won. Suppressed evidence changes everything.

**The fork -- plea or trial.** First-time offenders have leverage that repeat offenders don't:
- **Diversion programs** -- complete a program and charges are dismissed. Record stays clean.
- **Deferred adjudication** -- plead guilty, but conviction isn't entered if you complete probation.
- **Charge reduction** -- prosecutors often reduce felonies to misdemeanors for first-timers.
- **Withhold of adjudication** -- in some states, found guilty but no conviction on your record.

Ask your attorney specifically: "Am I eligible for diversion? Is deferred adjudication an option? Can we negotiate a charge reduction to a misdemeanor?"

**The five common mistakes:**
1. Talking to police without an attorney (often irreversible)
2. Not reading your own discovery (you know your life -- you'll catch things your attorney misses)
3. Accepting the first plea without investigation (first offer is almost never the best)
4. Assuming the worst (many first-time cases end in dismissal, diversion, or probation)
5. Not asking questions (your attorney works for you -- be a partner, not a passenger)

A felony charge is not the end. It's the beginning of a process. The more you understand that process, the better your outcome.
