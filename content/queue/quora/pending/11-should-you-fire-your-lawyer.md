## Quora: INNA-Q11 | Should I fire my criminal defense attorney?
**Topics**: Criminal Defense, Changing Attorneys, Attorney-Client, Defendant Rights, Legal Representation
**SEO**: "should I fire my lawyer" / "how to fire criminal defense attorney" / "changing lawyers during case"

You're lying awake at 2 AM thinking about this. Your court date is in six weeks. You've paid thousands. You have no idea what's happening with your case. Let me give you the framework.

**Fire them when:**

They're not communicating. You consistently can't reach them within 48 business hours. Not once -- consistently. If you've documented multiple attempts over weeks with no substantive response, that's grounds for a change.

They haven't done any work. No motions filed. No independent investigation. No strategy discussion. No discovery review with you. Just court appearances and continuances. If your case has been open for months and you can't point to a single concrete thing your attorney has actually done -- not said they'd do, actually done -- that's a problem.

They're pressuring you on the plea. "This is the best deal you're going to get" without explaining why. Refusing to discuss trial as an option. Getting visibly frustrated when you ask questions. Never mentioning alternative dispositions like diversion or drug court.

They missed a deadline. Missing a suppression motion deadline, a speedy trial demand, or a discovery request can permanently damage your case. This is potentially malpractice.

**Don't fire them when:**

You're unhappy with the news, not the lawyer. Sometimes the evidence is bad. A good attorney who tells you hard truths is better than a bad one who tells you what you want to hear.

You're close to trial. Switching 2-3 weeks before trial is extremely risky. The new attorney needs time to prepare.

Someone online told you to. Reddit is full of advice about firing lawyers. Make this decision based on your documented experience.

**How to do it right:**

1. Line up new counsel FIRST. Don't fire until you have someone new.
2. Request your complete case file -- discovery, motions, correspondence, notes. You have a right to it.
3. Review your retainer agreement for refund provisions. Attorneys must refund unearned retainer portions.
4. Notify your current attorney in writing. Professional, factual, no accusations.
5. New attorney files substitution of counsel with the court.

And the sunk cost trap: "I already paid $10,000." The money is already spent whether you stay or go. A new attorney at $7,500 who actually works your case is infinitely more valuable than a current attorney you paid $10,000 who doesn't. Your freedom is worth more than any retainer.
