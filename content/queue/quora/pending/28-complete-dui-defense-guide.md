## Quora: INNA-Q28 | What are the best defenses against a DUI charge?
**Topics**: DUI Defense, Breathalyzer, Field Sobriety Tests, Criminal Defense, DUI Arrest
**SEO**: "DUI defenses that work" / "how to fight a DUI" / "DUI defense strategies"

DUI cases are more technical than most people realize. The prosecution's case depends on a chain of procedures that all have to be done correctly -- the stop, the field sobriety tests, the breath or blood test, the arrest. Break one link in the chain, and the case starts falling apart.

Here are the main defenses that actually matter:

**The traffic stop has to be legal.** The officer needed reasonable suspicion -- a specific, articulable reason to pull you over. "Weaving within your lane" is debatable in many jurisdictions. "Driving slowly" is almost never enough. If the dashcam doesn't show what the officer claimed in their report, that's a suppression motion. Everything that happened after an illegal stop is fruit of the poisonous tree.

**Field sobriety tests are wrong 18% of the time.** That's according to NHTSA's own research -- the government agency that created the tests. And that's under perfect conditions on a flat, dry surface with clear instructions. Your test was conducted on a highway shoulder at midnight with flashing lights. Officers routinely deviate from NHTSA protocol: rushing the HGN eye test, skipping the medical history check, administering walk-and-turn on gravel. Every deviation is a crack in the prosecution's evidence.

**Breathalyzers have a margin of error.** Plus or minus .005 to .02 BAC depending on the device. If you blew .08 or .09, that margin alone could put you under the limit. Beyond that, the machine requires regular calibration and a certified operator. There's a mandatory 15-20 minute observation period before the test. If any of those were missed, the BAC result is challengeable. Your attorney can subpoena the calibration records for the specific device by serial number.

**Blood tests have their own problems.** Chain of custody breaks. Fermentation from improper storage (which produces alcohol that wasn't there when the blood was drawn). Contamination from alcohol swabs at the draw site. And the rising blood alcohol defense -- if your last drink was recent, your BAC while driving may have been lower than the BAC at the time of testing.

**Your attorney should be doing all of this.** Reviewing dashcam footage frame by frame. Comparing the police report to what the video actually shows. Subpoenaing calibration records. Evaluating FST administration against NHTSA standards. Filing suppression motions where warranted.

If your attorney's strategy is "let's see what they offer" -- that's not a defense. That's processing.

The most important thing to understand about DUI defense is that most cases are won or lost in the evidence review and motion stage, not at trial. A DUI attorney who files the right motions can gut the prosecution's case before it ever reaches a jury.

Ask your attorney: Have you watched all the dashcam and bodycam footage? Have you requested calibration records? Have you compared the police report to the video? Those questions will tell you everything you need to know about how your case is being handled.
