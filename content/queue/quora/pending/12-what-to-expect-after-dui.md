## Quora: INNA-Q12 | I just got arrested for DUI. What happens next?
**Topics**: DUI, DUI Arrest, Criminal Defense, License Suspension, First Offense DUI
**SEO**: "what happens after DUI arrest" / "first DUI what to expect" / "DUI arrest timeline"

You're reading this at 3 AM and your stomach is in knots. Here's the honest timeline.

**First 24-48 hours.** For most first-offense DUIs, you're released on your own recognizance or after posting bail. Right now, do these things: write down everything you remember about the stop and arrest (memory fades fast), save any GPS or timestamp data on your phone, don't post anything on social media, and don't talk to anyone about the case except an attorney.

**URGENT, your license.** In most states, you have 7-15 days to request a DMV administrative hearing to fight your license suspension. This is separate from the criminal case. Miss this deadline and your license gets automatically suspended, sometimes for months. This is the thing most attorneys don't mention until it's too late. Ask immediately: "Have you requested a DMV hearing?"

**Week 1, Finding an attorney.** Look for DUI specialization, not "I handle DUI among 30 other things." Ask during the consultation: How many DUI cases have you handled this year? What's your approach to challenging the traffic stop? Have you reviewed breathalyzer accuracy issues? What's the realistic best and worst outcome?

**The real cost nobody puts on their website:** Attorney fees ($2,500-$10,000), DMV hearing fees, court fines ($500-$2,000+), DUI school ($500-$1,500), increased insurance ($1,000-$3,000/year for 3-5 years). Total first DUI: $10,000-$25,000 over several years.

**Months 1-2, What your attorney should be analyzing:** Was the stop legal? Were field sobriety tests administered correctly per NHTSA standards? Is the BAC evidence reliable (calibration records, maintenance logs, operator certification)? Were your rights respected?

**Months 2-4, Motions and negotiations.** Motion to suppress (illegal stop or search). Motion to exclude BAC results (calibration or operator issues). Motion to dismiss (fundamental problems). Before you consider any plea: understand exactly what you're pleading to (misdemeanor DUI vs. reckless driving vs. wet reckless, very different consequences), know the full consequences beyond jail, and compare to trial odds.

**Best realistic outcomes (first offense, no accident):** Reduced to reckless driving, deferred prosecution/diversion, DUI conviction with minimal probation and no jail.

**The thing nobody tells you:** A first-offense DUI, while serious, is rarely the life-ending disaster you're imagining right now. Most people get through it. Many get reduced charges. Almost none go to prison for a first offense with no accident. You'll get through this.
