## Quora: INNA-Q07 | How do I know if my criminal defense attorney is actually working my case?
**Topics**: Criminal Defense, Attorney Accountability, Defendant Rights, Legal Representation, Red Flags
**SEO**: "is my lawyer doing anything" / "how to tell if attorney is working" / "criminal defense attorney red flags"

Here's the dirty truth about criminal defense: some attorneys take on too many cases, some collect the retainer and coast until the plea offer lands, and some are genuinely working hard but terrible at communicating. From where you're sitting, all three look exactly the same.

So here's how to tell what's actually happening.

**What a working attorney does within the first month:**
- Obtained discovery from the prosecution (or filed a demand for it)
- Reviewed the charging documents and identified potential issues
- Had an initial strategy conversation with you
- Identified suppression issues, was the stop legal? Was the search valid?
- Filed any time-sensitive motions (some deadlines are early and missing them is malpractice)

**The red flags:**

You can't get a return call within 48 hours. Consistently. Not once, consistently.

They can't tell you what motions they've filed. If you ask "what motions have you filed in my case?" and they stumble or say "we're not there yet" six months in, that's a problem. Every criminal case has potential motions.

They haven't reviewed discovery with you. You have a right to see the evidence being used to prosecute you. If your attorney says "don't worry about the discovery," that's a massive red flag.

They push the plea without explaining alternatives. "Just take the deal" is not legal strategy. It's laziness. A good attorney presents the plea, discusses trial odds, and lets YOU make an informed decision.

Nothing happens between court dates. The real work happens between hearings, investigation, motion drafting, witness interviews, legal research. If the only activity is showing up to court and asking for a continuance, your attorney is not working.

Every court date is a continuance. One or two can be strategic. Five in a row with no explanation? They're stalling because they haven't done the work.

**What to do:**

Put it in writing. Send your attorney an email with specific questions: What is the current status? What motions have been filed? Have you reviewed all discovery? What is your strategy? When can we schedule a case review?

A professional attorney responds substantively. An attorney who's been coasting either ignores it or gives you fluff. Either response tells you what you need to know.
