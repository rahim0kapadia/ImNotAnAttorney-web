## Quora: INNA-Q17 | How often should my criminal defense attorney communicate with me?
**Topics**: Attorney Communication, Criminal Defense, Legal Ethics, Defendant Rights, ABA Model Rules
**SEO**: "how often should lawyer communicate" / "attorney communication standards" / "lawyer not updating me"

The ABA Model Rule 1.4 says attorneys must "keep the client reasonably informed" and "promptly comply with reasonable requests for information." The word "reasonably" does a lot of heavy lifting there. Here's what it should actually look like.

**Minimum communication standards from attorneys who get good outcomes:**

After initial meeting: within 1 week with a case strategy overview. Discovery received: within 2 weeks with a summary. Before any court date: at least 48 hours ahead. After any court date: same day or next day. Motion filed or ruled on: within 24 hours. Plea offer received: immediately, this requires YOUR decision. Routine update with no activity: at least once per month.

Return calls within 24-48 business hours. Respond to emails within 48-72 business hours. Urgent matters: same day.

**What most defendants actually experience:**

"I paid $15,000 and I haven't spoken to my attorney in 2 months."
"I found out about my court date from the court's automated system, not my lawyer."
"My attorney's paralegal told me to be patient. I've been patient for 8 months."

These aren't outliers. This is common.

**Why communication breaks down:** Too many cases (the math of 60-100+ active cases doesn't allow for meaningful communication with every client). They're doing work but not telling you about it (still a failure, just a different kind). Nothing is happening and they don't want to admit it. They've mentally moved on to the next retainer.

**What to do:**

Set expectations early. At your next meeting: "Can we agree on monthly status updates, 48-hour callback windows, and same-day notification for any plea offers or major developments?"

Send structured update requests instead of open-ended "what's happening" calls. Email specific questions: What is the current status of discovery? Have any motions been filed? Has there been any communication with the prosecution? What are the upcoming deadlines?

Escalate if needed. Level 1: specific written request. Level 2: certified letter requesting communication within 10 days. Level 3: formal case review meeting. Level 4: contact the state bar's client assistance program. Level 5: consult with a new attorney.

You're not being "difficult" by expecting communication. You're being a responsible participant in your own defense.
