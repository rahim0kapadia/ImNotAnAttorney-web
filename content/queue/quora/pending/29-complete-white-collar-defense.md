## Quora: INNA-Q29 | What should I know if I'm facing federal white collar charges?
**Topics**: White Collar Crime, Federal Defense, Wire Fraud, Federal Sentencing, Criminal Defense
**SEO**: "white collar defense guide" / "facing federal charges" / "federal fraud defense"

Federal white collar cases are a different universe from state criminal cases. Everything moves slower, the discovery is more massive, the sentencing guidelines are more complex, and the conviction rate is above 90%. Understanding the terrain is the first thing that matters.

**The investigation started long before you knew about it.** Federal investigations run for months or years before targets are notified. By the time you get a target letter or the FBI shows up, they've already pulled bank records, subpoenaed emails, and probably talked to people you work with. They're not investigating -- they're confirming.

**The pre-indictment window is where you have the most leverage.** Experienced white collar attorneys often engage with prosecutors before charges are filed, presenting evidence or arguments for why charges shouldn't be brought. Once the indictment drops, you're playing defense. Before it drops, you might prevent it entirely.

**Loss amount drives everything at sentencing.** Federal sentencing guidelines for fraud start with a base offense level and add enhancements -- and the loss amount is usually the biggest one. The difference between $240,000 and $260,000 in alleged losses can mean 2 additional sentencing levels, which can translate to months or years of additional prison time. Your attorney should be challenging every dollar of the government's loss calculation.

**Cooperation is a strategic decision, not a surrender.** The government may offer a proffer session -- a "queen for a day" meeting where you provide information under a limited immunity agreement. But proffer agreements have major exceptions. If you lie about anything, the entire agreement is void and everything you said becomes admissible. If you testify at trial and contradict your proffer statements, they can use them to impeach you. The golden rule: if you go in, you go in all the way. Half-truths will destroy you.

**Brady material gets buried in volume.** White collar cases sometimes involve millions of documents in discovery. The prosecution is required to turn over evidence favorable to your defense, but finding exculpatory material in that haystack requires electronic discovery tools and systematic review -- not a quick skim of the highlights.

**Questions for your attorney:**

- What is the government's calculated loss amount, and do you agree with it?
- What sentencing departures or variances are available?
- Has the government produced all Brady and Giglio material?
- Is a proffer advisable, and what are the specific risks?
- What is the realistic sentencing range with cooperation vs. without?

The defendants who get the best outcomes in federal cases are the ones who understand the process, challenge the numbers, and ask the right questions early -- not the ones who panic and sign whatever the government puts in front of them.
