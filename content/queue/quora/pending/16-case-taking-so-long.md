## Quora: INNA-Q16 | Why is my criminal case taking so long?
**Topics**: Criminal Case Timeline, Court Delays, Continuances, Criminal Defense, Defendant Rights
**SEO**: "criminal case taking too long" / "why is my court case delayed" / "criminal case timeline how long"

There's a critical difference between a case that's taking long because your attorney is working and one that's taking long because nobody is doing anything.

**Normal timelines for comparison:**
- Simple misdemeanor: 1-4 months
- DUI (first offense): 3-6 months
- State felony: 6-18 months
- Federal felony: 12-24+ months
- Complex multi-defendant: 18-36 months (this is actually normal)

**Legitimate reasons for delay:**

Massive discovery. In drug, fraud, and federal cases, discovery can be thousands of pages. Reviewing it takes time. How to tell it's legitimate: your attorney can tell you specifically what they're reviewing.

Strategic delay in your favor. Witnesses' memories fade. Evidence degrades. Co-defendants take deals that change your position. How to tell: your attorney can articulate WHY delay helps you.

Motions being litigated. A single suppression motion can take 3-6 months from filing to ruling. How to tell: check the court docket for actual filings.

Court backlog. Post-COVID, many courts have massive backlogs. Ask your attorney: "Is the delay from our side, the prosecution's, or the court's scheduling?"

**Red flags the delay means nothing is happening:**

Every court date is a continuance with no explanation. Five in a row with no work done between them means your attorney is stalling.

Your attorney can't tell you what's happening. "These things take time" is not an answer. A real answer: "We filed a motion to suppress on January 15th. The prosecution's response is due February 28th."

No motions have been filed after 6+ months. Even cases that resolve by plea should have motion activity, motions create leverage for better plea deals.

You're the one initiating every communication. If the only time you hear from your attorney is when you call them, your case isn't their priority.

**What to do:** Check the court docket yourself (search "[your county] court records"). Request a written timeline from your attorney. Ask about your speedy trial rights, and whether they've been waived through continuances without your knowledge.

The waiting is its own punishment. You can't plan, can't move, can't make decisions. You deserve a timeline and straight answers.
