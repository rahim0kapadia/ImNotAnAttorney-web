## Quora: INNA-Q08 | What questions should every criminal defendant ask their lawyer?
**Topics**: Criminal Defense, Defendant Rights, Attorney-Client, Legal Strategy, Criminal Law
**SEO**: "questions to ask criminal defense attorney" / "what to ask your lawyer" / "defendant questions for attorney"

The quality of your questions determines the quality of your defense. Most defendants hire an attorney and go quiet. They wait. They trust. Then critical deadlines pass, motions don't get filed, and their options narrow to whatever plea the prosecutor offers.

Here are the ten questions that change that dynamic.

**1. "What is your theory of defense?"** This is the most important question. A theory of defense is the story your attorney is telling -- to the prosecutor, the judge, a potential jury. If they say "we'll see what they offer," they don't have one. And if they don't have one, they're not defending you. They're processing you.

**2. "Have you reviewed all of the discovery?"** Every page? Every video? If not, why not? And can you see it? You lived the events. You'll catch things your attorney might miss.

**3. "What motions are you planning to file?"** Suppress illegally obtained evidence. Compel the prosecution to turn over what they're hiding. Challenge unreliable expert testimony. If your attorney says none are needed, ask them to explain why each specific motion doesn't apply.

**4. "What does the prosecution have to prove, element by element?"** Every charge has elements the State must prove beyond a reasonable doubt. If they can't prove even one, they can't convict. Ask which element is weakest. That's where your defense lives.

**5. "What is the worst realistic outcome, and what is the best?"** Not the theoretical maximum. Not the minimum. The realistic range based on your facts, your judge, your jurisdiction.

**6. "Have you spoken to the prosecutor about my case specifically?"** Negotiation happens in conversations -- informal ones. If your attorney only interacts with the prosecution at scheduled court dates, they're not fighting. They're waiting.

**7. "What independent investigation have you done?"** Visited the scene? Interviewed witnesses? Checked calibration records? Examined expert credentials? If the only investigation was done by the people trying to convict you, that's not a defense.

**8. "What are the weaknesses in the prosecution's case?"** Every case has them. An attorney who can't identify a single weakness hasn't looked hard enough.

**9. "If we go to trial, what's your plan?"** Even if you end up taking a plea, your attorney needs a trial strategy. The strength of your plea negotiation is directly tied to the prosecution's fear of losing at trial.

**10. "What should I be doing right now to help my case?"** Character letters, treatment programs, employment documentation. A good attorney gives you a specific list. A bad one says "just stay out of trouble."

Send these in advance by email. A good attorney prepares real answers. A bad one dodges or gets defensive.
