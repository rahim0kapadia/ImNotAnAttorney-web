## Quora: INNA-Q32 | What are the stages of a criminal case and how long does each one take?
**Topics**: Criminal Process, Court Proceedings, Criminal Defense, Arraignment, Plea Deals
**SEO**: "stages of criminal case" / "how criminal cases work" / "criminal case timeline"

Nobody hands you a map. You get arrested, you get a court date, and then you're supposed to figure out the rest while your entire life is on hold. Here's how it actually works.

**Stage 1: Arrest and Booking.** You're fingerprinted, photographed, and processed. This takes hours to a day. The most important thing to know: anything you say from the moment of arrest can be used against you. The right to remain silent isn't a formality. Officers are trained to keep conversations going. "Friendly conversation" at booking is still evidence collection.

**Stage 2: First Appearance / Bond Hearing.** Within 24-72 hours, you go before a judge. They inform you of the charges and set bail. If you can't make bail, you sit in jail while your case plays out, which can take months. Being detained affects outcomes: detained defendants accept plea deals at higher rates because jail is miserable and a plea means going home.

**Stage 3: Arraignment.** You formally enter a plea, almost always "not guilty" at this stage, even if you plan to negotiate later. Pleading not guilty preserves all your options. This hearing is procedural, usually 5-15 minutes. It's not where you present a defense.

**Stage 4: Discovery.** The prosecution is required to share their evidence. Police reports, witness statements, lab results, videos, phone records, everything they've gathered. This is one of the most important stages of the entire case. Discovery is where you find out what the prosecution actually has and where their case is weakest. Inconsistencies between the officer's report and the dashcam footage. Missing evidence that should exist. Constitutional violations in how evidence was collected.

**Stage 5: Motions.** Your attorney files formal legal requests, to suppress illegally obtained evidence, to dismiss defective charges, to compel the prosecution to turn over Brady material. This is where most cases are actually won. Not at trial. A successful suppression motion can remove the prosecution's key evidence. Without that evidence, the case often collapses.

**Stage 6: Plea Negotiations.** About 90-97% of criminal convictions come from guilty pleas, not trials. The strength of your plea deal depends on the work done in stages 4 and 5. An attorney who actually filed motions and found evidence problems has leverage. An attorney who did nothing is negotiating from a position of weakness.

**Stage 7: Trial.** Only 3-10% of cases get here. Cases are decided by the preparation that came before, motions, witness preparation, cross-examination strategy. Trial is not like television.

**Stage 8: Sentencing.** If convicted, the court imposes your sentence. A presentence investigation report is prepared. Your attorney should review it for errors, inaccuracies in the PSI can affect your sentence.

**Timelines:**
- Simple misdemeanor: 2-6 months
- State felony: 6-18 months
- Complex state felony: 1-3 years
- Federal felony: 1-4 years

The defendants who navigate this best aren't the ones who trust the process blindly. They're the ones who understand each stage, know what their attorney should be doing at each one, and ask specific questions when they don't see it happening.
