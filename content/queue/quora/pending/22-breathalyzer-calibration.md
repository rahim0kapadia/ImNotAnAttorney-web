## Quora: INNA-Q22 | Can I challenge my breathalyzer results in a DUI case?
**Topics**: DUI Defense, Breathalyzer, BAC Testing, Criminal Evidence, DUI Dismissal
**SEO**: "challenge breathalyzer results" / "breathalyzer calibration records" / "DUI BAC evidence unreliable"

The number on the breathalyzer isn't a fact. It's an opinion from a machine.

A machine that needs regular calibration, operated by a person who needs current certification, that sat in a patrol car through summer heat and winter cold, with an error margin prosecutors never mention. Your BAC result feels like a verdict. It's a data point -- and its value depends entirely on how it was collected.

**Four things your attorney should be challenging:**

**Calibration records.** Breathalyzers drift. Sensors degrade, get contaminated by environmental factors, and produce readings that skew higher or lower. Every department is supposed to calibrate these devices on schedule -- typically every 30, 60, or 90 days. If the last calibration was 47 days ago on a 30-day cycle, every reading since day 30 is questionable. Your attorney should subpoena 12 months of calibration records for the specific device (by serial number).

**Operator certification.** The person who administered your test needs to be certified on the specific make and model used. Not "breathalyzer operation generally." Certified on THAT machine. Certifications expire. If the certification lapsed before your test date, the result was administered by an uncertified operator.

**The observation period.** Before you blow, the operator is supposed to watch you continuously for 15-20 minutes. No eating, drinking, smoking, vomiting, or belching -- because residual mouth alcohol produces a reading that reflects your mouth, not your blood. This is the single most common procedural violation in DUI breath testing. Ask your attorney: was the officer actually watching me during those 15 minutes, or were they doing paperwork?

**The margin of error.** Most breath testing devices have an accepted margin of plus or minus 0.005 to 0.02 BAC. A reading of .08 could actually be anywhere from .06 to .10. If you blew .08 or .09, the margin of error alone could put you under the legal limit. Additionally, breathalyzers don't measure blood alcohol directly -- they measure breath alcohol and multiply by a standard ratio (2100:1) that varies significantly between individuals.

**What your attorney should be requesting in discovery:** Calibration records (12 months), maintenance and repair logs, operator certification records, the observation period log, the device's operator manual, quality assurance records, and any error codes or aborted tests from your testing session.

If the prosecution can't produce these records, or produces records showing deficiencies, every gap is a weakness in their case.
