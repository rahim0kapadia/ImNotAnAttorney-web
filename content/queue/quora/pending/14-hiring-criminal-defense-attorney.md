## Quora: INNA-Q14 | What should I ask before hiring a criminal defense attorney?
**Topics**: Hiring Attorney, Criminal Defense, Legal Fees, Attorney Consultation, Choosing a Lawyer
**SEO**: "how to choose criminal defense attorney" / "questions before hiring lawyer" / "criminal defense consultation questions"

You're about to hand someone $5,000-$25,000 to defend your freedom. Most people pick based on a Google search and a bail bondsman's recommendation. Here's how to actually evaluate them.

Treat the consultation as YOUR interview of THEM.

**"How many cases like mine have you handled?"** Not "how many criminal cases" -- your specific type. A specific number with context is good: "About 40 drug possession cases in this county over the past two years." "I handle all kinds of criminal cases" is a red flag. That's a generalist, not a specialist.

**"What's your approach to my type of charge?"** Specific strategies are good: "I typically start by challenging the traffic stop and requesting calibration records." Generic platitudes like "I fight hard for all my clients" mean nothing.

**"Who actually works on my case?"** In larger firms, you might meet the partner at the consultation and get assigned a first-year associate. Know who's doing the work.

**"What's your communication policy?"** How quickly do you return calls? How often will I receive updates? If the answer is "just call my office" -- that's not a policy, that's the beginning of a communication nightmare.

**"What do you see as the realistic outcome?"** Not the best-case fairy tale. Not the worst-case scare tactic. If they say "I can get this dismissed" before reviewing a single piece of evidence, they're selling, not advising.

**"What's your trial experience?"** Prosecutors know which attorneys will actually try a case and which always plead out. If your attorney has a reputation for folding, the prosecution offers their worst deals.

**"What's the total cost, and what does the retainer cover?"** The trap: a low retainer that only covers "standard representation" (i.e., the plea deal). Filing motions, trial, expert witnesses -- all extra. Get it in writing.

**Red flags during the consultation:**
- They guarantee an outcome (no attorney can do this)
- They pressure you to sign immediately
- They badmouth other attorneys
- They get annoyed when you ask questions
- The office is chaos -- messy desk, phone ringing off the hook, multiple interruptions

After you choose, send a follow-up email: "I want to confirm our agreement on communication -- you'll return calls within 24 hours and provide updates before each court date. I'll be an active participant in my defense and want to review all discovery."

This creates a paper trail from day one.
