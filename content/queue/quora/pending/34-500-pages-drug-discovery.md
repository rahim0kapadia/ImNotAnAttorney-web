## Quora: INNA-Q34 | What kind of evidence is in drug case discovery? Should I read my own discovery?
**Topics**: Drug Cases, Discovery Documents, Criminal Evidence, Drug Trafficking, Defendant Rights
**SEO**: "drug case discovery documents" / "reading your own discovery" / "evidence in drug trafficking case"

Yes. Read your own discovery. You might be surprised what you find.

I know about a case where a defendant facing drug trafficking charges in Florida decided to open his own 500-page discovery file after months of hearing "we're working on it" from his attorney. No legal training. Just patience and attention to detail.

He found four critical issues his attorney had never mentioned.

**A 73% weight discrepancy.** Officers weighed the substance at the scene: 93.9 grams. The lab weighed it at 25.59 grams. That's 68.3 grams that disappeared somewhere between the scene and the lab. In a drug trafficking case, weight determines everything, the charge level, the mandatory minimum, the sentencing guidelines. 93.9 grams and 25.59 grams can mean the difference between trafficking charges and simple possession. His attorney had not raised it.

**A phone number attributed to two different people.** The investigation relied on a confidential informant. In the police report, a specific phone number was listed as the CI's number, used to set up controlled buys. In the same report, that same number was also attributed to the defendant. One number, two people, same document. The entire narrative of how the defendant was allegedly involved depends on correctly attributing communications. His attorney had not questioned it.

**The wrong drug.** Officers wrote "amphetamine" in their reports. The charge was based on amphetamine. The lab results confirmed MDMA/MDA. These are not the same substance, different chemical composition, potentially different scheduling, different legal consequences. His attorney had not raised the mismatch.

**21 fingerprints, zero matches.** Law enforcement collected 21 latent fingerprints from the evidence. Not a single one matched the defendant. In a case alleging he possessed and trafficked drugs, nothing physically connected him to the substance. His attorney had not mentioned this.

**Why this happens:** Overworked attorneys with flat-fee billing structures. Once the retainer is paid, every hour spent reviewing discovery is an hour worked for free. The financial incentive is to negotiate a plea as quickly as possible. That doesn't make it acceptable. It makes it something you need to protect yourself against.

**What to look for in your discovery:**

- Do the weights match between scene and lab?
- Does the charged substance match what the lab confirmed?
- Is the physical evidence actually connected to you, fingerprints, DNA, phone records?
- Are the informant records internally consistent, phone numbers, dates, locations?
- Does the search warrant hold up, was there probable cause?

You don't need a law degree. You need patience and the willingness to ask questions. You lived the events in question. You will spot things in those documents that a busy attorney might miss.
