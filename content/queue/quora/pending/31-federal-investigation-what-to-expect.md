## Quora: INNA-Q31 | The FBI contacted me about an investigation. What should I do?
**Topics**: Federal Investigation, FBI, Target Letter, Criminal Defense, White Collar Crime
**SEO**: "FBI contacted me what to do" / "federal investigation target" / "target letter federal case"

Stop. Do not talk to them. That is the single most important thing you can do right now.

I know that sounds dramatic. The agents were probably polite. They probably said something like "we just want to understand what happened" or "you're not in trouble, we just need your help." Every single word is designed to keep you talking.

Here's what's actually happening: by the time federal agents show up at your door, they've been investigating for months -- sometimes years. They've pulled your bank records. They've subpoenaed your emails. They've talked to your associates. They're not investigating. They're confirming what they already believe. And they want you to fill in the gaps.

Under 18 U.S.C. 1001, lying to a federal agent is a separate felony -- even if the underlying investigation goes nowhere. And "lying" includes misremembering a date, misstating a detail, or getting confused in a stressful doorstep conversation. People have been convicted of 1001 charges when the original investigation was dropped.

**The 48-hour playbook:**

**Hours 0-4:** Say five words -- "I want to speak with my attorney" -- and then stop talking. You don't need to explain. You don't need to clarify. You invoke your right to counsel and you're done.

**Hours 4-12:** Hire a federal criminal defense attorney. Not your cousin who does DUIs. Not your business attorney. Someone who has tried cases in federal court, knows the AUSAs in your district, and has handled cases like yours. Ask them: How many federal criminal cases have you handled? How many have gone to trial vs. plea? Do you have experience with the specific charges I might be facing?

**Hours 12-24:** Preserve everything, destroy nothing. The moment you know about a federal investigation, deleting an email, shredding a document, or "cleaning up" your files becomes potential obstruction of justice under 18 U.S.C. 1519 -- which carries up to 20 years. People have beaten fraud charges and gone to prison for the cover-up.

**Hours 24-48:** Work with your attorney to map your exposure. What do they likely have? What's your realistic sentencing range? Who else might be cooperating? Is a proffer worth considering?

**What the government classifies you as matters:**

A "witness" has relevant information but isn't suspected of a crime. A "subject" is in the gray zone -- conduct falls within the scope but no charging decision yet. A "target" means they have substantial evidence and are likely seeking an indictment.

If you received a target letter, the government is telling you they're coming. Federal prosecutors don't send these casually.

The timeline from here is long. Federal cases take 1-5 years from investigation to resolution. The decisions you make in these first 48 hours set the trajectory for everything that follows. Get an attorney. Shut your mouth. And don't touch a single document.
