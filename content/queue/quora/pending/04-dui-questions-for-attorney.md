## Quora: INNA-Q04 | What questions should I ask my DUI lawyer?
**Topics**: DUI, Criminal Defense, Attorney Accountability, DUI Defense, Legal Rights
**SEO**: "questions to ask DUI attorney" / "DUI lawyer questions" / "hold DUI attorney accountable"

Five questions. That's all it takes to find out whether your DUI attorney is actually working your case or just waiting for the plea offer to land.

**1. "Was the traffic stop itself legal?"**

Officers need reasonable suspicion to pull you over. Not a hunch. Not a feeling. Articulable facts. "Weaving within your lane" is debatable -- courts are split on whether that's sufficient. "Driving 2 mph under the speed limit" is almost never enough. If the stop was illegal, everything that followed -- field sobriety tests, breathalyzer, arrest -- can potentially be suppressed. If your attorney hasn't analyzed this, ask why.

**2. "Have you reviewed the calibration records for the breathalyzer?"**

This is the one that separates DUI attorneys who actually fight from those who coast. Breathalyzer machines need regular calibration -- typically every 30, 60, or 90 days depending on the model. If the machine was overdue for calibration, the BAC number the prosecution is waving around might not hold up. But someone has to actually request and review those records. Most attorneys don't bother.

**3. "What motions have you filed -- or plan to file?"**

If the answer is "none" or "we'll see," that's a red flag. Motion to suppress the stop. Motion to exclude BAC results based on calibration issues. Motion to compel dashcam and bodycam footage. Motion to challenge the field sobriety test administration against NHTSA standards. There are always motions to consider in a DUI case. If your attorney says motions aren't necessary, ask them to explain specifically why each one doesn't apply to your case.

**4. "Can I see the dashcam and bodycam footage?"**

You have a right to see the evidence against you. Period. If your attorney hasn't requested this footage, or won't show it to you, that's information about how your case is being handled. The footage often tells a different story than the police report -- officers' written descriptions of field sobriety test "failures" frequently don't match what the camera shows.

**5. "What's your actual strategy for my case?"**

"Wait for the plea offer" is not a strategy. That's surrender. A real defense strategy examines every angle: the legality of the stop, the field sobriety test administration, the chemical test procedures, the officer's training records, the observation period, the chain of custody. If your attorney can't articulate a plan, you deserve to know that now -- not six months in when all the motion deadlines have passed.

Write these down before your next meeting. Email them in advance so your attorney can prepare real answers. The quality of the questions you ask directly determines the quality of your defense.
