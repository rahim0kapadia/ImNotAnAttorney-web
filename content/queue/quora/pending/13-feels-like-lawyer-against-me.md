## Quora: INNA-Q13 | Why does it feel like my defense lawyer is working against me?
**Topics**: Criminal Defense, Attorney-Client Conflict, Defendant Rights, Public Defender, Legal Representation
**SEO**: "lawyer working against me" / "defense attorney not fighting" / "attorney pushing plea deal"

You're sitting in the hallway outside the courtroom and your attorney just told you to take the deal. Again. For the third time. Without discussing alternatives. Without explaining why trial isn't an option.

This feeling, that your own lawyer is working against you, is one of the most common things defendants experience. And sometimes, you're right.

**Why it happens:**

They took too many cases. When someone has 80-100 active cases, your file becomes a number. The path of least resistance is always the plea deal, faster, easier, frees them up for the next retainer. The economics of their practice don't align with your interests.

They've already decided you're guilty. Some attorneys, consciously or unconsciously, start working toward a plea the moment they see the charges. They don't investigate. They don't file motions. They treat the prosecution's evidence as gospel.

They're afraid of the judge or prosecutor. In small jurisdictions, defense attorneys who file too many motions can develop reputations as "difficult." Some attorneys prioritize their courthouse relationships over your defense.

**But also, sometimes it's a communication problem.** The evidence might genuinely be bad. The plea deal might be the best realistic option. Your attorney might be doing everything right but terrible at explaining why. Before concluding the worst, make sure you've had a real strategy conversation.

**The warning signs that it's more than communication:**

They agreed to terms without consulting you. If your attorney agreed to anything, a continuance, a plea framework, without discussing it with you first, that's a violation of their duty.

They spend more time with the prosecutor than with you. Professional relationships are normal. But if your lawyer is laughing and chatting with opposing counsel while you're sitting there terrified, that dynamic is broken.

They haven't done any independent work. Check the court docket online. If the only filings are appearance entries and continuance requests, your attorney hasn't been working.

**What to do:**

Ask direct questions: "What is your theory of defense? What motions have you considered? What are the specific weaknesses in the prosecution's case? If this were your family member, would you recommend taking this plea?"

Write down the answers. Then check the court docket and compare what they say to what's actually been filed. If there's a gap between promises and reality, you have your answer.
