## Quora: INNA-Q23 | Are field sobriety tests accurate? Can they be challenged?
**Topics**: DUI Defense, Field Sobriety Tests, NHTSA Standards, Criminal Evidence, DUI Dismissal
**SEO**: "field sobriety test accuracy" / "challenge field sobriety test DUI" / "NHTSA FST standards"

The government's own research says field sobriety tests are wrong at least 18% of the time -- even when administered perfectly. Officers rarely administer them perfectly.

NHTSA validates only three tests: Horizontal Gaze Nystagmus (HGN), Walk-and-Turn, and One-Leg Stand. Anything else -- finger-to-nose, alphabet backward, counting -- is not standardized, not validated, and not supported by NHTSA research. If an officer used non-standardized tests as evidence of impairment, that's already a problem.

**The accuracy numbers from NHTSA's own studies:**
- HGN: 77% accurate at identifying BAC over .10
- Walk-and-Turn: 68% accurate
- One-Leg Stand: 65% accurate
- All three combined: 82% accurate at BAC over .08

That means even with perfect administration, these tests misidentify sober people as impaired 18-35% of the time depending on the test.

**What officers get wrong:**

On the HGN test: rushing through passes (NHTSA says 2 seconds each -- officers frequently do 1 second), holding the stimulus too high (forcing head tilt that causes nystagmus unrelated to alcohol), never asking about medical conditions (at least 38 conditions cause nystagmus without alcohol, including inner ear disorders, certain medications, even caffeine).

On Walk-and-Turn: administering on uneven or sloped surfaces (NHTSA requires "reasonably dry, hard, level, non-slippery" -- highway shoulders with gravel aren't that), abbreviated instructions, not offering to let you remove shoes with heels over 2 inches.

On One-Leg Stand: not actually timing the 30 seconds (many officers estimate -- letting you stand 40-50 seconds means even a sober person might put their foot down), same surface issues, and the massive stress factor. You've been pulled over by armed officers with flashing lights. Stress causes trembling and swaying that have nothing to do with alcohol.

**The dashcam tells the truth.** Officers write their reports after the arrest. The dashcam footage shows what actually happened. Common discrepancies: officer reports "subject stepped off the line 3 times" but footage shows the "line" was imaginary on gravel. Officer reports "subject could not maintain balance" but footage shows normal weight shifting.

If the FSTs were the basis for probable cause to arrest you, suppressing them can unravel the entire evidence chain. No valid FSTs means no probable cause. No probable cause means no lawful basis for the breath test. The BAC result gets excluded too.
