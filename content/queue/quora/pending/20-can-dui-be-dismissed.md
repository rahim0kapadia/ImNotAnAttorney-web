## Quora: INNA-Q20 | Can a DUI case be dismissed? What are the actual defenses?
**Topics**: DUI Defense, DUI Dismissal, Breathalyzer, Field Sobriety Tests, Criminal Defense
**SEO**: "can DUI be dismissed" / "DUI defenses that work" / "get DUI case thrown out"

DUI cases get dismissed all the time. Not because of TV-style legal tricks, but because of real, specific errors that police officers make during traffic stops, field sobriety tests, and chemical testing.

Here are the defenses your attorney should be investigating:

**1. The traffic stop was illegal.** The Fourth Amendment requires reasonable suspicion. The officer needs a specific reason, not a hunch. "Weaving within your lane" is debatable. "Driving slowly" is almost never enough. If the stop was illegal, everything after it (tests, breathalyzer, arrest) can be suppressed.

**2. Field sobriety tests were administered incorrectly.** NHTSA has strict protocols. The government's own research says these tests misidentify sober people as impaired 18% of the time even when administered perfectly. Officers commonly rush through them, administer them on uneven surfaces, skip the medical history check, or miscount clues. If the dashcam shows deviations from NHTSA standards, the results can be challenged.

**3. Breathalyzer results are unreliable.** The machine needs regular calibration. The operator needs current certification on that specific model. There's a required 15-20 minute observation period before testing. And breathalyzers have a margin of error of plus or minus 0.005-0.02 BAC. If you blew .08 or .09, the margin of error alone could put you under the legal limit. Your attorney should be subpoenaing calibration records, operator certification, and observation period logs.

**4. Blood test errors.** Chain of custody issues. Fermentation from improper storage (which produces alcohol that wasn't there at the time of the draw). Contamination from alcohol swabs at the draw site. Timing, your BAC may have been below .08 while driving and only rose above it by the time you were tested (rising blood alcohol defense).

**5. No probable cause for arrest.** Even if the stop was legal, the officer needs probable cause for the arrest itself. The smell of alcohol alone isn't enough in many jurisdictions. Alternative explanations for observed behavior, fatigue, medication, medical conditions, are legitimate challenges.

**6. Miranda and right to counsel violations.** Questioned about drinking after arrest without Miranda warnings? Your statements may be suppressed.

**What your attorney should be doing right now:** Reviewing dashcam and bodycam footage, analyzing the stop's legality, evaluating SFST administration against NHTSA standards, subpoenaing breathalyzer calibration records, checking officer certification, and filing suppression motions for any violations found.

If your attorney's only strategy is "let's see what they offer", they're not doing their job.
