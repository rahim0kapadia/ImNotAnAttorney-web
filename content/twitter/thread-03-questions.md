The quality of your defense is directly related to the quality of your questions.

Most defendants ask nothing. They trust. They wait. They hope.

Here are 10 questions that change everything (🧵):

---

1/ "What is your theory of defense?"

Every case needs a story — a framework that explains why you should win.

Good answer: Specific strategy. "We're arguing the stop was illegal."

Bad answer: "We'll see what they offer." That's surrender, not defense.

---

2/ "Have you reviewed all of the discovery?"

Discovery is every piece of evidence against you. Your attorney MUST have read it.

Follow up: "Can I see it?" You have the right.

If they refuse or dodge — serious problem.

---

3/ "What motions have you filed — or plan to file?"

Motions are how attorneys fight. Suppression motions can throw out evidence. Brady motions force disclosure.

If zero motions exist and they can't explain why each one doesn't apply — they haven't looked.

---

4/ "What are the elements of my charges, and which one is weakest?"

Every charge has elements the prosecution must prove. If they can't prove ONE, they can't convict.

Your attorney should know exactly which element is vulnerable.

---

5/ "What is the realistic best and worst outcome?"

Not theoretical maximums. Not minimums.

REALISTIC numbers based on your facts, your judge, your jurisdiction.

If they can't give you a range — they haven't thought about your case.

---

6/ "What independent investigation have you done?"

The police investigated. The prosecution investigated. What has YOUR attorney done?

Scene visits. Witness interviews. Evidence review. Calibration checks.

If the answer is "nothing" — you're defending yourself with only the prosecution's work.

---

7/ "Have you spoken directly to the prosecutor?"

Negotiation happens in conversations. Your attorney should be talking to the assigned prosecutor.

If they only see the prosecutor at court dates — they're not negotiating. They're waiting.

---

8/ "What are the weaknesses in the prosecution's case?"

Every case has weaknesses. Every single one.

If your attorney can't identify a single vulnerability — they haven't looked hard enough.

Push for specifics. Get names, dates, issues.

---

9/ "If we go to trial, what's your plan?"

Even if you plead, your attorney needs a trial strategy.

The strength of your plea deal is tied to the prosecution's fear of losing.

No trial plan = no leverage = worst possible deal.

---

10/ "What should I be doing right now?"

Good attorneys give you specific actions: character letters, treatment programs, employment documentation.

Bad attorneys say "just stay out of trouble."

If your attorney can't give you homework, they're not leading your defense.
