# Thread: Red Flags Your Attorney Is Failing You

The #1 complaint to state bars against criminal defense attorneys:

📞 "They won't return my calls."

Not malpractice. Not incompetence. Just... neglect.

Here are 9 red flags your attorney is failing you (🧵):

---

1/ Red Flag #1: You can't reach them.

One unreturned call is bad. Three is a pattern. A month of silence is a crisis.

Attorneys are busy. But returning calls within 48 hours is basic professionalism.

If you consistently can't get through, you're not a priority.

---

2/ Red Flag #2: They can't tell you what motions they've filed.

Ask specifically: "What motions have you filed?"

Vague answers like "we're working on it" or "it's not the right time" mean nothing has been filed.

Every case has potential motions. If zero exist, they haven't looked.

---

3/ Red Flag #3: They haven't reviewed discovery with you.

Discovery is the evidence. It's your case. You have a RIGHT to see it.

If your attorney says "don't worry about that" or "you don't need to see it" — that's a violation of your right to know.

Demand access. Read it yourself if needed.

---

4/ Red Flag #4: They push the plea deal without alternatives.

"Just take the deal" is not legal strategy.

A good attorney presents the offer, explains the pros and cons, discusses trial risk, and lets YOU decide.

If they pressure you to plead quickly without analysis — they're managing their schedule, not your defense.

---

5/ Red Flag #5: They don't know basic facts about your case.

If your attorney mixes up your charges, confuses you with another client, or asks you to remind them about key details — they have too many cases.

Your case isn't getting attention. You're getting processed.

---

6/ Red Flag #6: Nothing happens between court dates.

The real work happens between appearances — investigation, motion drafting, witness interviews.

If the only activity on your case is showing up and asking for continuances, nothing is being done.

Ask specifically: "What work has been done since the last court date?"

---

7/ Red Flag #7: Every court date is a continuance.

One or two continuances can be strategic. Five or six with no explanation?

Your attorney is stalling. They haven't done the work. And now they're buying time.

Ask directly: "Why do we need another continuance? What work is happening?"

---

8/ Red Flag #8: They dismiss your concerns.

You ask a reasonable question. They say "don't worry about it" or "you don't understand how this works."

That's condescension, not representation.

A good attorney respects your right to know, even when explaining things takes longer.

---

9/ Red Flag #9: They can't articulate a theory of defense.

Ask: "What's our theory of defense?"

If they stumble, get vague, or say "we'll see what they offer" — they don't have one.

Every case needs a story. If your attorney can't tell you what it is, they're not defending you.

---

10/ The hard truth:

Most attorneys aren't evil. Most are just overwhelmed.

But the result is the same — you're paying for a defense you're not getting.

The solution?

Ask the hard questions. Document everything. And if the answers don't improve, it might be time for a change.
