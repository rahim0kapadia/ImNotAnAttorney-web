I'm fighting a felony case.

I paid $20,000 to a private attorney. He never called me back. No motions filed. No discovery reviewed. Just "trust the process."

3 months in, I still didn't understand my own case.

So I started researching.

Here's what I found (🧵):

---

1/ I discovered something weird:

The attorney had access to my discovery for 3 months. He never read it.

I read it myself. In one afternoon.

Found issues he never mentioned:
- Weight discrepancies
- CI attribution errors
- Officer statement conflicts

My attorney was getting paid to do nothing.

---

2/ The system is broken for defendants.

You pay someone to defend you. You have no idea if they're working. You can't evaluate their work. You can't read the discovery. You don't know what motions should be filed.

You're trusting someone with your freedom while being completely in the dark.

---

3/ I started asking questions.

"What motions are you filing?"
"We don't need any."

"What did you find in discovery?"
"You don't need to worry about that."

"Your theory of defense?"
"We'll see what they offer."

These aren't answers. These are deflections.

---

4/ The questions changed everything.

I started asking SPECIFIC questions:

"When was the last calibration on the breathalyzer?"
"What's the chain of custody on the drugs?"
"What witness statements conflict with the officer report?"

Suddenly my attorney had answers. And deadlines. And work product.

---

5/ Here's what I learned:

A good attorney WANTS informed clients. They welcome questions. They explain things.

A bad attorney, or an overwhelmed one, hates questions. Because questions require answers. And work.

The quality of your defense is directly related to the quality of your questions.

---

6/ I built a system.

I analyzed tactics from 40+ elite defense attorneys. I learned what good defense looks like. I created frameworks for evaluating my own case.

And I found things my $20K attorney missed.

Every single time I asked informed questions, something changed.
