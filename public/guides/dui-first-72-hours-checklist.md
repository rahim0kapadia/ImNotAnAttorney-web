## Do These Three Things Now

### 1. Request Your DMV Hearing TODAY

Your deadline is **7 to 30 days from arrest** depending on your state. The clock is already running. If your attorney hasn't done this, call them now.

This is separate from your criminal case. Miss this deadline and your license gets automatically suspended, regardless of what happens in court. There is no "I didn't know" exception. There is no extension.

**DMV Hearing Request Deadlines by State:**

| Deadline | States |
|----------|------, |
| **7 days** | Colorado (breath/refusal), New York, Washington |
| **10 days** | California, Colorado (blood test), Florida, Indiana, Oregon |
| **14 days** | Michigan |
| **15 days** | Arizona, Missouri, Texas |
| **30 days** | Georgia, Ohio, South Carolina |
| **No separate admin hearing** | Kentucky, Montana, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Dakota, Tennessee, license suspension handled through court process |

*This table reflects state statutes as of early 2026. Confirm your state's current deadline with your attorney or your state's DMV/DOR website on the day of your arrest. If your state isn't listed, call your state DMV immediately.*

### 2. Write Down Everything You Remember About the Stop

Tonight. Not tomorrow. Your memory fades fast.

Write down:
- What time were you pulled over?
- What reason did the officer give for the stop?
- What road conditions existed (lighting, weather, road surface)?
- What did the officer say to you? What exact words?
- Were you asked to take field sobriety tests? Which ones? On what surface?
- How long did you wait before a breath or blood test?
- Were you read your rights? When?
- Was there a dashcam or bodycam? Did you see one?

This becomes your case journal. Even if you never use it in court, it's invaluable for your attorney meeting.

## What NOT to Do, Common Mistakes That Hurt Your Case

**Do not post about your arrest on social media.** Nothing. No vague posts. No check-ins at the courthouse. Prosecutors have internet access. Anything you post can and will be used against you.

**Do not talk about your case with anyone except your attorney.** Not friends. Not coworkers. Conversations with your attorney are privileged. Conversations with everyone else are not. Your friend can be subpoenaed.

**Do not go back to the bar or restaurant to ask for security footage.** This is evidence tampering territory. Your attorney handles evidence collection through proper legal channels.

**Do not call the arresting officer.** Not to apologize. Not to ask for leniency. Nothing you say will help you. Everything you say can be used against you.

**Do not ignore the citation or the DMV notice.** Both contain deadlines. Missing a court date results in a bench warrant. Missing the DMV deadline results in automatic license suspension.

**Do not drive on a suspended license.** If your license was confiscated and you're driving on a temporary permit, verify the expiration date. A driving-on-suspended charge on top of a DUI dramatically worsens your case.

## 6 Questions for Your Attorney Consultation

Bring these to your first meeting. Write down the answers.

### 1. "Have you requested my DMV hearing?"
If they look confused, that's information. The DMV hearing has its own deadline and is separate from your criminal case. An attorney who hasn't mentioned it may not be treating your case as urgently as it requires.

### 2. "What is your theory of defense?"
An attorney without a theory is waiting for the plea offer. A good answer names specific evidence and identifies a path. A bad answer says "we're still early in the process" or "let's see what the state offers."

### 3. "Have you reviewed the dashcam footage?"
Dashcam and bodycam footage is often the most important evidence in a DUI case. If your attorney hasn't requested it, ask why.

### 4. "Were the field sobriety tests administered correctly?"
NHTSA has strict protocols for field sobriety tests. If the officer improvised, wrong surface, wrong instructions, wrong scoring, the results can be challenged.

### 5. "What's your plan for the breathalyzer evidence?"
Breathalyzers need regular calibration. Blood samples need proper chain of custody. Your attorney should be requesting calibration logs and maintenance records.

### 6. "What's the realistic best and worst outcome?"
Vague optimism is worse than honest assessment. A good attorney can articulate the range, from dismissal to conviction, and explain what drives the outcome in each direction.
