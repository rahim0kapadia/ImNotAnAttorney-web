# OG Link-Preview Copy Critique — Suby × Hormozi × Atti Cascade

Date: 2026-04-16
Scope: 32 OG previews under `src/app/**/opengraph-image.tsx`
Lenses: Sabri Suby (magnetic headlines, cost-of-inaction), Alex Hormozi (value equation, specificity), Atti (UPL guardian, crisis-buyer architect, pro-defendant positioning)

---

## 1. The Central Diagnosis (one sentence)

**The copy is brand-voice talking to itself, not defendant-fear talking to a 2AM thumb — every hero title names what WE sell ("Judge Report Card", "Similar Cases Analyzer", "Defense Playbooks") instead of what the defendant is terrified of right now, which Suby calls "category-name-as-headline" (the #1 conversion killer), Hormozi calls "feature-naming instead of dream-outcome-naming", and Atti would reject as failing the 3AM panic test.**

The second-order failure: every subtitle describes process ("sentencing patterns, prosecutor pairing data, and bench vs. jury divergence") when the defendant does not know those words exist. They know one word: **scared**.

## 2. Eight Flagship Rewrites

Target: 1–2 lines, under ~35 chars/line at Playfair 88px, scannable in 0.8s, names a specific fear or dream outcome, passes UPL and anti-cliche.

### Root homepage
- **Current title:** `Know,\nWhat They Know.`
- **Current category:** `Legal Intelligence`
- **Current subtitle:** "Defense intelligence for criminal defendants. Close the information gap with the prosecution."

**Keep the title.** This one is earned — the tagline IS the hook (Atti positioning precision). But the category "Legal Intelligence" buries the buyer. Change category and tighten subtitle.

- **Rewrite title:** `Know\nWhat They Know.` *(drop the comma — Suby: no drag on the eye)*
- **Rewrite category:** `For the defendant`
- **Rewrite subtitle:** "Every other person in the courtroom already does. You're the stranger. Close the gap."

*Suby lens:* the courtroom-stranger frame is the magnetic hook. *Hormozi:* dream outcome = not being the only one in the dark. *Atti:* pro-defendant, not anti-attorney.

### Judge Report Card
- **Current title:** `Judge Report Card`
- **Current category:** `Defense Intelligence`

The current title is a product name — Hormozi: "you named the category, not the outcome." A scared defendant doesn't care what a Judge Report Card is; they care whether their judge is going to destroy their life.

- **Rewrite title:** `Who Is\nYour Judge?`
- **Rewrite category:** `Know before you walk in`

*Suby:* question headlines + curiosity gap outperform declarative every time. *Hormozi:* "dream outcome = walking into court not blind." *Atti:* lawful intel, no UPL, and no "beat the judge" language (which would read anti-system).

### Similar Cases Analyzer
- **Current title:** `Similar Cases Analyzer`
- **Current category:** `Defense Intelligence`

"Analyzer" is a tool name. The buyer's question is: *what happened to people like me?*

- **Rewrite title:** `What Happened\nto People Like You.`
- **Rewrite category:** `Outcomes from cases like yours`

*Suby:* specificity is a magnet — "people like you" forces the reader to picture themselves. *Hormozi:* perceived likelihood-of-success rises the moment they see a match pattern. *Atti:* factual, no prediction, no guarantee — just "what happened."

### Arrest Survival Kit

Title is fine but redundant with category. The subtitle "Know your rights before they read you yours" is the real hook — it belongs upstairs.

- **Rewrite title:** `Know Your Rights\nBefore They Read Them.`

*Suby:* the inversion ("before they read yours") is the magnetic pattern-break. *Hormozi:* time-delay collapses to zero — the value lands pre-arrest or mid-arrest. *Atti:* insider-voice, not corporate-safety.

### DUI Checklist (free)

Not bad but flat. "Free Resource" is Suby's cardinal sin — leading with what WE are giving instead of what THEY lose by not acting. The DMV 10-day deadline is the magnetic hook this product is sitting on.

- **Rewrite category:** `After a DUI arrest`

*Suby:* deadline + loss-frame = magnetic headline formula. *Hormozi:* makes the cost of NOT acting concrete (losing license). *Atti:* passes UPL — a deadline is a fact, not advice.

### Plea Deal Analyzer (free)

- **Current category:** `Free Tool`

The title is already question-format (good Suby) but "fair" is the wrong verb — plea-offer buyers are not asking about fairness, they're asking *am I getting played?*

*Suby:* "actually a deal" = curiosity-gap loaded with implied answer ("no"). *Hormozi:* cost of inaction = signing away your life in minutes. *Atti:* pro-defendant, not anti-prosecution, and respects the 94% plea reality the project already blogs about.

### Sample X-Ray
- **Current title:** `Sample X-Ray Report`
- **Current category:** `Sample Report`

"Sample Report" sounds like a catalog page, not a conversion asset. The buyer's question: *what do I actually get for $2,497?* The preview of the deliverable IS the reverse-risk close.

- **Rewrite category:** `Real case, redacted`

*Suby:* price-upfront in a headline is a contrarian magnet on a premium offer — only works when paired with proof. *Hormozi:* "show, don't tell" = reduces perceived risk, raises perceived likelihood. *Atti:* "turn art into science" — the sample is the rigor proof.

### Blog hub

"Blog" is a label that died in 2012. The blog's real job = long-tail SEO + trust-building for crisis Googlers. The category name should match crisis intent, not CMS category.

## 3. The Verb Rule

**Imperative "know" verbs (and their cousins: see, check, read) as the default verb family. Questions ("is your…", "who is…") as the aggressive alternative. Zero abstract nouns as titles.**

Why: the tagline is already "Know What They Know." Every preview should feel like a drumbeat of that tagline. Suby's research on magnetic headlines shows imperatives + questions out-convert declaratives by ~30%; Hormozi insists the dream outcome be named as a verb-state ("know", "see") not a product feature; Atti's UPL guardrail forbids action-verbs like "fight", "win", "beat", "challenge" in titles — "know" is the UPL-safe imperative that still drives urgency.

Banned verb families in titles: **get** (weak, passive-aggressive freebie signal), **find out** (Buzzfeed-era), **learn** (school-marm), **discover** (MLM), **unlock** (SaaS cliche), **master** (bro), any form of "fight" / "beat" / "win" (UPL risk + anti-attorney read).

## 5. What NOT to Say in an INAA Preview (5 bullets)

1. **Never a word ending in -izer, -analyzer, -checker, -dashboard.** Tool-names in headlines are conversion death. Name the question the tool answers instead.
2. **Never "free" in the headline on a crisis page.** Suby: "free" cheapens the signal on a high-stakes decision. Keep "Free" for the stat row only — never the title.
3. **Never "your attorney" as a foil.** Crosses into anti-attorney positioning. Use "the courtroom", "the system", "the prosecution" as the information-gap opponent, never the defense lawyer.
4. **Never a numeric price in a title except on sample/proof pages.** Price in a headline reads transactional. The one exception is `sample-xray` where the price IS the proof move.
5. **Never verbs that imply legal action: fight, win, beat, defend, challenge, overturn, suppress.** UPL failure mode. We are information, not counsel. Use: know, see, read, check, prepare.

## 6. Trust-Signal Integration

**Yes, but only on 4 specific preview types, and only in the `stat` row — never the title.** Suby's "don't crowd the hook" rule: the title carries one job (emotional pull); the stat row carries proof. Mixing them dilutes both.

Where to place numeric signals:

- **judge-report-card:** `$197 · 15,000+ federal judges` — keep. The number earns the price.
- **district-court-intelligence:** `$197 · 94 federal districts` — keep.
- **sample-xray / sample:** add a rigor number. Suggested: `Real case · 47-page excerpt` (or whatever the actual page count is — verify before shipping).
- **similar-cases-analyzer:** add outcome-data scale. Suggested: `$297 · [N] federal sentences analyzed` (pull from JUSTFAIR 595K count — that number is the whole moat).

Where NOT to place numeric signals:

- **Crisis pages** (arrest-survival-kit, dui-checklist, family, plea-analyzer, score). A defendant in panic cannot process "10 questions · 2 minutes" — they process "before you sign." Hormozi: specificity works only when the buyer is rational enough to evaluate it. Crisis buyers are not rational buyers.
- **Brand pages** (home, about, start). The tagline IS the trust signal.
- **Partner pages.** The value is relational, not numeric.

**The risk of numeric signals on a preview trying to sell:** numbers without context read as bragging, and bragging in a crisis context is tone-deaf. "15,000+ federal judges" works because federal-judge-scale is a known universe — the defendant's lawyer can't match that. "10 questions · 2 minutes" reads as a quiz, not a lifeline. Atti's filter: would a defendant scrolling at 2AM feel served or sold-to? If sold-to, delete the number.

## The Pattern Behind the Rewrites

Every rewrite above inverts the same failure: the current copy says **what it is**; the rewrite says **what you're afraid of not knowing**. Suby built a career on that inversion. Hormozi wrote the value equation on it. Atti's entire persona is the institutional memory that the defendant is the only stranger in the room. When the three lenses agree, the rewrite is close to right. When they disagree, the Atti UPL lens wins by default — because on a criminal-defense preview, one hallucinated promise destroys more than ten lost clicks earn.
