### 3b. Defense Theories

These are established categories of defense inquiry for borderline-BAC DWI cases. Your attorney can determine which apply to your facts.

**Instrument Precision Challenge (Taylor/McShane methodology)**
Breathalyzers are measurement instruments with a documented margin of error — typically ±0.005 to ±0.02. A 0.09 reading on an instrument with a ±0.01 margin means the true BAC could be anywhere from 0.08 to 0.10. At 0.08, you're at the legal limit — not above it. Whether this defense applies depends on the specific breathalyzer model, its calibration history, and the operator's certification. These are facts in the discovery file that your attorney can request and review.

**Rising BAC Defense**
BAC continues to rise after the last drink is consumed. If there was a meaningful delay between when you were driving and when the breath test was administered, your BAC at the time of driving may have been below 0.08. This is a factual question based on the timeline — when did you last drink, when were you stopped, and when was the test administered?

**Field Sobriety Test Validity (Head methodology)**
William "Bubba" Head's approach examines whether SFSTs were administered according to NHTSA standards. Even small deviations from the standardized protocol — wrong instructions, uneven surface, inadequate demonstration — affect reliability. Body camera footage is critical here because it shows exactly how the tests were conducted.

**Traffic Stop Legality**
Every DWI case begins with a traffic stop. If the officer lacked reasonable suspicion for the stop, a motion to suppress can exclude everything that followed — including the BAC test. The police report and body camera footage document the officer's stated reason for the stop.
